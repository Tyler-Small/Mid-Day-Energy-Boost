# Win Utah HOA Managers With Small Jobs

Focus on Wasatch Front HOA management companies, specifically the portfolio community managers at mid-size firms and national branch offices that coordinate outside vendors, and sell them a guaranteed small-repair vendor whose paperwork and invoices clear their software on the first try. Add rental property managers as a second, separately marketed account type in months four to nine (65% certain). Do not market to homeowners directly and do not build the plan around $200 resident TV mounts: homeowner leads cost $130 to $200 per won customer against a $40 fee, and a $200 mount nets roughly $20 to $28 after card fees and admin, so resident work is a byproduct of the manager relationship, never the business (85% certain). HOA managers and rental PMs are different professions with different trade bodies, licenses, software and payment chains, so one campaign aimed at both will read as generic to each. Tyler's belief that HOA managers are the more reliable payers is half right: associations are solvent and audited but slow (board approval cycles, paper checks), while homeowners pay at point of service and the real B2C risk is card disputes, not nonpayment (75% certain). The "3,400 HOAs" figure matches the state registry (3,364 registrations in October 2025, 2,997 of them sub-HOAs), but the number of real buyers is a few dozen management companies plus self-managed boards. Twenty management clients are plausibly worth $100K to $450K a year in gross margin under stated assumptions, with the low end far more likely in year one (40% certain). The biggest hole in the current plan is the margin model itself: a 20% coordination fee cannot fund dispatch, insurance, a guarantee and 30 to 60 days of float. The business lives on the labor spread (bill $85 to $110 an hour, pay $45 to $60) and on the guarantee as the thing managers pay a premium for (70% certain).

## Holes and errors in Tyler's current thinking

1. **"HOA managers pay more reliably than homeowners."** Wrong on speed, right on solvency. More than 70% of HOA vendor payments were still paper checks in the AvidXchange data (dated, 2018), board-signature requirements cause "significant delays," and management-company AP guides warn "a simple bill approval can take several weeks" ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/?p=505633); [Clark Simson Miller](https://clarksimsonmiller.com/bill-and-invoice-payments/)). Residential contractors are paid within 30 days 48% of the time versus 21% on public work, and online payments passed 50% of all Jobber transactions in Q3 2025 ([Pro Builder](https://www.probuilder.com/construction/labor-trade-relations/article/55219423/residential-contractors-more-likely-to-be-paid-on-time-than-commercial-contractors); [Landscape Management](https://www.landscapemanagement.net/jobber-releases-its-latest-home-service-economic-report-for-q3/)). Homeowners pay faster; associations pay more surely (75% certain).
2. **Treating HOA managers and rental PMs as one segment.** They have separate trade bodies, credential ladders, software, Utah licensing treatment and payment chains, and CAI actively lobbies to keep them legally separate ([CAI press room](https://www.caionline.org/press-room/the-profession-of-community-association-manager/)). One message for both lands as "another handyman" to each (90% certain).
3. **Selling a "liaison."** Managers do not buy coordination layers; the management company is already paid to coordinate, and no standalone HOA maintenance coordinator exists anywhere in the US that the research could find ([liaison benchmarks: Inframark](https://www.inframark.com/solutions/community-management/arizona/association-management/vendor-contract-oversight/)). To the buyer, Tyler is a vendor with a guarantee. Keep "liaison" as the internal model and embed the 20% in the job price the way Lula does ([Lula Core](https://lula.life/core)) (80% certain).
4. **20% on $200 jobs funds a business.** It nets about $20 to $28 per job. $5,000 a month of net fee income needs roughly 200 jobs a month, which is a dispatch operation, not a side margin. The economics live in the labor spread and the guarantee premium (85% certain).
5. **Float is missing from the model.** Paying 1099 handymen on completion while associations pay in 30 to 60 days means financing 100% of job value to earn 20%. Lula built "Fast Pay" and Jobox an embedded credit line to solve exactly this ([Lula Core](https://lula.life/core)) (80% certain).
6. **"Land and expand" to residents.** The only quantified case is Amenify in multifamily, self-reported, and its own numbers imply roughly 0.13 to 0.2 services per household per year across every category ([Amenify](https://amenify.com/blog/amenify-builds-service-pro-network-for-both-residents-and-operators)). HOA boards and legislators treat preferred-vendor programs with suspicion ([HOA Insights podcast](https://www.reservestudy.com/164-preferred-hoa-vendors-may-not-be-your-friend/); [MN Legislature testimony](https://www.lcc.mn.gov/cichoa/meetings/20241206/Kathleen-Phillips-Powerpoint)). Treat it as free upside, never as a revenue line (75% certain).
7. **3,400 HOAs equals 3,400 buyers.** The registry count is 3,364, but 2,997 are sub-HOAs, mostly managed by the same handful of firms, and the biggest Utah firm (FCS, ~200 communities) runs its own 12-person maintenance crew ([Utah Commerce presentation](https://www.utah.gov/pmn/files/1486081.pdf); [FCS profile](https://jobs.caionline.org/profile/fcs-community-management/202678/)). Buyers number in the dozens (85% certain).
8. **Compliance is a fixed cost, not a checkbox.** $1M per occurrence / $2M aggregate GL with each association and the management company named as additional insured, workers' comp, W-9, matching legal names across all three, and a one-third failure rate among vendor applicants on paperwork ([HOAMCO](https://hoamco.com/vendor-services/); [FSR Texas](https://www.fsresidential.com/texas/news-and-events/articles/ask-the-expert-vendor-management)). Vantaca holds payment by default until these clear ([Vantaca support](https://support.vantaca.com/hc/en-us/articles/360025047351-How-to-Set-Up-or-Edit-a-Service-Provider)).
9. **Utah law assumptions.** The handyman exemption appears to be $7,000 per project since HB 483 (2024), with a DOPL affirmation of exemption for $3,000 to $7,000 and electrical, plumbing and HVAC always licensed; the Service Marketplace Platforms Act presumes independent-contractor status only for jobs priced at $3,000 or less ([HB 483](https://le.utah.gov/~2024/bills/hbillamd/HB0483S01.htm); [Utah Code 34-53](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title34/Chapter53/C34-53-S102_2018050820180508.pdf)). The clean 1099 envelope is jobs under $3,000 (70% certain; statute text seen only in excerpts).
10. **Copy that sounds like everyone else.** "Partner," "one call," "licensed and insured," "we treat your community like our own" appear on every competitor page, including Associa's "Your Partner in Community Success" ([Associa Utah](https://click.associaonline.com/csshoa/)).
11. **Calendar.** The 2026 UCCAI vendor trade show already happened (April 30, 2026). The event inside the next 90 days is NARPM Utah's November conference, which serves the rental PM tier, not HOAs ([UCCAI events](https://uccai.com/events); [NARPM Utah supplier application](https://assets.noviams.com/novi-file-uploads/uaa/NARPM/SUPPLIERS_NARPM_UTAH_CHAPTER_Application.pdf)).

## HOA managers and rental PMs are different professions with a four-tier market

CAI defines the community association manager as working for the owners' collective body (budgets, board reports, covenant enforcement, authorizing association payments) and hired by a board vote, while the property manager "focuses on individual units" and is hired by an individual owner ([CAI HOA Resources](https://hoaresources.caionline.org/how-community-managers-and-property-managers-have-distinct-roles/)). The credential ladders differ (CMCA, AMS, PCAM versus RMP, MPM, CRMC), the trade bodies differ (CAI with **50,000+ members** versus NARPM with **~6,000**), and in Utah the licensing now diverges: rental PM requires a real-estate license or the new stand-alone property-manager license (effective January 1, 2027 per the Division of Real Estate, though one tracker says May 2026), while a 2025 bill "specifically exempts common interest association managers" ([Utah DRE](https://commerce.utah.gov/realestate/property-management/); [CAI Utah EOS 2025](https://www.caionline.org/getmedia/c73d664a-8616-40df-9722-0f53440e123a/UTEOSReport2025.pdf)). The software splits the same way: Vantaca and CINC are HOA-only and skew to mid-market firms (Vantaca claims **550+ management companies and 6.5 million doors**), while AppFolio and Buildium are rental-first and 86% small-business ([Vantaca Vision 2025](https://www.wxow.com/online_features/press_releases/vantaca-unveils-next-gen-ai-powered-intelligent-community-management-ecosystem-at-vision-2025/article_6901f60a-7825-54f5-8b57-9f78ef3f9216.html); [G2](https://g2crowd.com/categories/community-association-management)).

The pain is different in kind. Rental PM pain is operational and measured daily: the 2025 operator average from work order to invoice slipped to **6.9 days** against a 6.8-day satisfaction tipping point, **84% of renters expect a response within 24 hours**, and Utah's Fit Premises Act gives a landlord **3 calendar days** to fix a habitability defect ([Property Meld](https://propertymeld.com/blog/what-we-learned-in-vendor-management-in-2025/); [Buildium Renters Report](https://www.buildium.com/resource/2026-renters-report/); [Utah Code 57-22-6](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title57/Chapter22/C57-22-S6_2023050320240701.html)). HOA pain is administrative and fiduciary: **71% of community managers report moderate-to-severe burnout, 80% have considered leaving, 97% of firms report a manager shortage**, and the modal manager carries 8 to 10 communities while FCS in Utah runs about 14 per manager ([ManageCasa on FCAR](https://managecasa.com/articles/hoa-manager-burnout-retention); [FCAR Benchmarking 2025](https://foundation.caionline.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/MgmtBenchmarking2025.pdf); [FCS profile](https://jobs.caionline.org/profile/fcs-community-management/202678/)). A PM buys speed. An HOA manager buys fewer bids to chase, fewer insurance certificates to track, and invoices that do not bounce.

The tiering, in order of fit for a third-party small-repair vendor, is: small-to-mid rental PM firms with no in-house techs (steady flow, one decision-maker, tenant clock); professionally managed HOAs reached through the management company's vendor list (compliance-heavy, slower, but 10 to 200 associations per relationship); self-managed boards, which are **30 to 40% of US associations** but tiny and slow ([CAI Statistical Review 2025](https://foundation.caionline.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/2025StatisticalReviewFoundation.pdf)); and institutional multifamily, which staffs roughly one maintenance tech per 100 units and buys only overflow and turnover help ([NAA staffing](https://www.naahq.org/sites/default/files/naa_bestpractices_satffing.pdf)). On pure volume the rental PM tier ranks first, and every durable venture-backed coordinator (Latchel, Lula, Property Meld) chose it. For the "scale off of sameness" goal, the HOA tier ranks first because the position is empty in Utah, the buyer population is small enough to reach in full, and the small-job problem is documented and unowned (65% certain). A minority of Utah firms do both (Maxfield, Wasatch Leasing, Vision, Advantage, Welch Randall, Treo), and the attached-housing boom (townhomes and condos went from 15% to 55% of permitted units between 2013 and 2022) means more rentals sit inside HOAs, so the same townhome can produce an HOA exterior ticket and a PM interior ticket ([BiggerPockets Provo](https://www.biggerpockets.com/ut/provo/property-managers?page=1); [Salt Lake Tribune](https://sltrib.com/news/2024/02/29/utah-builders-have-had-boom-town)). Run two campaigns on one back office.

## Utah has about 3,400 registered associations and a few dozen real buyers

The "3,400" figure is sound as a registry count: Utah Commerce reported **3,364 total registrations (367 master HOAs, 2,997 sub-HOAs)** in October 2025, with Salt Lake City, Park City and St. George the top cities ([Utah Commerce presentation](https://www.utah.gov/pmn/files/1486081.pdf)). CAI's estimates run higher at **3,650 to 3,800 associations housing 208,800 homes and about 644,000 Utahns**, roughly 57 homes per association, which signals a long tail of small townhome and condo projects with no staff ([CAI Utah EOS 2025](https://www.caionline.org/getmedia/c73d664a-8616-40df-9722-0f53440e123a/UTEOSReport2025.pdf); [CAI Statistical Review 2025](https://foundation.caionline.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/2025StatisticalReviewFoundation.pdf)). More than **58% of new Utah listings in 2025 carried HOA fees** ([Axios SLC](https://www.axios.com/local/salt-lake-city/2026/03/30/utah-hoa-market-share-dues-increasing)). Registration is now mandatory with annual renewal, a $90 fee, and loss of lien rights for non-compliance, and the public search at services.commerce.utah.gov/hoa is a free, current prospect list that includes management contacts ([Utah Commerce](https://commerce.utah.gov/2025/09/12/all-utah-hoas-must-re-register-now-in-the-new-state-system/)) (90% certain on process, 60% on how complete the contact fields are).

The buyer count is far smaller. FCS Community Management (HOA Living) serves about **200 communities with 14 managers and 12 maintenance staff and holds a GC license**, so it competes inside its own portfolio but still averages 17 communities per tech ([FCS profile](https://jobs.caionline.org/profile/fcs-community-management/202678/)). Welch Randall oversees 60+ associations from Ogden to Lehi, Model HOA manages 11 in Park City, and national branches (FirstService Residential, Associa via Community Solutions & Sales in Draper, CCMC at Daybreak, RealManage, Spectrum, Clark Simson Miller) plus mid-size locals (Advantage in Orem, HOA Strategies in Kaysville, PMI Reliant in Orem, Treo, CoreSolutions, Utah HOA Management) round out the field ([IREM Brad Randall](https://irem.org/about-irem/leadership-hq-team/brad-randall); [TownLift Model HOA](https://townlift.com/business/model-hoa/); [PMI Reliant](https://www.orempropertymanagementinc.com/lehi-association-management)). Even FCS holds perhaps 5% of associations, so the market is fragmented and a vendor needs many firms. The CAI Utah chapter has **about 320 members** and its 2025 Northern Utah vendor trade show sold out at **$500 a booth** ([GuideStar](https://www.guidestar.org/Profile/26-0024942); [UCCAI trade show](https://uccai.glueup.com/event/132108)). Demand is still being built: Daybreak's 190-unit Pennant opens fall 2026, The Point in Draper broke ground with a 363-unit first phase, Utah City in Vineyard covers 700 acres, and three northern Utah County cities took 18% of statewide permits in 2024 ([Daybreak](https://www.daybreakutah.com/whats-happening/a-healthly-plan/from-vision-to-reality-the-future-of-daybreak-living/); [Utah Legislature briefing](https://le.utah.gov/interim/2025/pdf/00002885.pdf)).

Revenue from 20 management clients is a scenario, not a finding. Nationally the average association has about 78 units and collects about $4,300 per unit in assessments, so roughly $335K per association; 20 clients at an assumed 24 associations each is 480 associations and about **$161M in assessments, of which 60 to 70% is operating budget** ([FCAR 2025](https://foundation.caionline.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/2025StatisticalReviewFoundation.pdf); [Gerald citing NerdWallet](https://joingerald.com/learn/money-basics/hoa-money)). No source isolates handyman-addressable repair from landscaping, insurance and utilities; if it is 5 to 10% of operating spend (assumption) and Tyler captures 10 to 20% of it, coordinated spend is **$0.5M to $2.3M a year, or $100K to $450K gross margin at 20%, equal to 1,000 to 5,000 work orders a year** (2 to 10 per association). Per client that is $5K to $23K of gross margin a year. The 24-associations-per-client assumption is unverified, and Tyler will not be the only vendor at any firm, so year one lands at the bottom of that range or below it (40% certain on the range, 70% certain year one is under $150K).

## Associations pay surely but slowly; homeowners pay fast but cost too much to win

HOA money moves through a manager-to-board-to-accounting chain, with each association a separate legal entity needing its own billing profile and invoices coded operating versus reserve, and payments "delayed for several days between each stage" ([LeapAP](https://www.leapap.com/blog/condo-hoa-accounts-payable-template); [InvoiceDataExtraction](https://invoicedataextraction.com/blog/hoa-accounts-payable)). Two-signature thresholds commonly sit at $1,000 to $5,000, and signers who act only at monthly meetings push payment to the next cycle ([BiggerPockets HOA funds](https://www.biggerpockets.com/member-blogs/3963/47355-preventing-theft-of-your-hoa-funds)). Once approved, Vantaca Vendor Pay disburses by **virtual card instantly, ACH in 1 to 5 business days, or check in 7 to 10** ([Vantaca FAQ](https://www.vantaca.com/vantaca-faq/which-community-association-management-software-has-the-best-vendor-portal)). The practical rule: keep each invoice under the manager's own approval cap, enroll once in the management company's AP platform, and bill per association by name. Nonpayment risk is low (assessment-funded, audited, two-signer controls); the risk is a 30 to 60 day receivable on a 20% margin.

Rental PMs pay from the owner's maintenance reserve within a contractual threshold and bill tenants for tenant-caused damage, so authorization is same-day or next-day for sub-threshold work and payment depends on reserve funding and PM discipline ([Bay Management Group](https://www.baymgmtgroup.com/blog/do-property-managers-pay-for-repairs-heres-the-truth/)). The repeat-business claim holds here more than in HOAs: a 300-door firm at **~2.2 work orders per unit per year** produces about 650 tickets a year at a few hundred dollars each, versus lumpy common-area projects on the HOA side ([Lula cost guide citing Bay PMG](https://lula.life/articles/rental-property-maintenance-costs-guide)). The catch is that PMs already mark up vendor invoices 10 to 20% or charge $25 to $50 per work order, and owners call it "double-dipping," so a visible 20% liaison fee stacks to 30 to 40% unless it is buried in a flat-rate price ([Bluefield Group](https://www.bluefieldgroup.com/blog/a-transparent-guide-to-property-management-fees-what-landlords-should-expect/)).

B2B in general carries measurable risk: half of US B2B invoices are overdue and 5 to 8% of B2B credit sales are written off, and **59% of small businesses have invoices 30+ days overdue** ([Atradius 2024](https://atradius.us/reports/payment-practices-barometer-b2b-payment-practices-trends-united-states-2024.html); [QuickBooks 2026](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/r/small-business-data/small-business-late-payments-report-2026/)). Homeowners are the opposite profile: fast pay, but home-services chargebacks reportedly approach the **1% monitoring threshold with a 20% merchant win rate**, so the mitigation is card on file at booking, charge at completion, and photos of finished work ([SwipeSimple](https://home.swipesimple.com/connect/blog/chargeback-prevention-for-home-services-hvac-plumbing-electrical-landscaping); [Chargeback.io](https://www.chargeback.io/it/blog/how-often-do-merchants-win-chargeback-disputes)). Mature handyman shops report 50 to 65% of revenue from repeat clients, but those are seller listings, not surveys ([BizQuest](https://www.bizquest.com/business-for-sale/high-demand-home-repair-business-immediate-income/BW2467390/)). So the "20 managers means more repeat and lower nonpayment" thesis is right about nonpayment, right about repeat for the PM tier, and wrong about cash speed for the HOA tier (75% certain).

On the townhome question: the Census counts a townhouse as single-family (attached) only if each unit has a ground-to-roof wall, its own heating and utility meters and no units above or below, and it treats "condominium" as a form of ownership, not a structure ([Census SOC definitions](https://www.census.gov/construction/soc/definitions.html)). Legally a condo owner holds the interior airspace and the HOA owns the exterior, while a fee-simple townhome owner holds the land and exterior unless the CC&Rs assign them to the association ([Opendoor](https://www.opendoor.com/articles/pros-and-cons-of-buying-condo-or-townhome)). For Tyler the operative question is who pays for the exterior, and in Daybreak-style sub-associations the sub-HOA fee covers snow, yard and "some exterior maintenance" ([Daybreak HOA](https://www.daybreakutah.com/whats-happening/daybreak-residents-2/daybreak-hoa)). Townhome owners are homeowners for marketing; scope follows the CC&Rs (85% certain).

## Position as the small-job vendor whose invoices clear, never as a "partner"

Only one handyman brand in the metro has a dedicated HOA page: Mr. Handyman of Lehi, Provo and Spanish Fork (4.9 stars, 365 reviews), pitching a single provider for exterior work, priced "by the job, not the hour" with no published rates ([Mr. Handyman HOA page](https://www.mrhandyman.com/lehi-provo-spanish-fork/about-us/hoa-maintenance/); [Mr. Handyman Utah](https://www.mrhandyman.com/locations/utah/)). Everyone else is a residential independent mentioning property managers in passing, an exterior contractor calling itself "HOA Specialists," or a national manager's in-house maintenance arm ([OneSource](https://1sourceutah.com/); [Associa Utah](https://click.associaonline.com/csshoa/)). Nobody in the metro positions as a vendor that guarantees independent handymen's work for community managers. The documented, unowned pains are: contractors who will not come out for a $200 fix ([HomeAdvisor](https://homeadvisor.com/r/?p=10515)), vendor no-shows and "communication gaps [that] lead to missed appointments, incomplete work" (that quote is from FCS's own blog, so it is a Utah manager's framing) ([HOA Living blog](https://www.hoaliving.com/blog/2026/07/15/when-to-hire-a-community-management-company/)), and paperwork holds in Vantaca ("Pending Insurance Documentation," "Awaiting W-9") ([Vantaca support](https://support.vantaca.com/hc/en-us/articles/360025047351-How-to-Set-Up-or-Edit-a-Service-Provider)). Hinge's growth research found high-growth firms 75% more likely to be highly specialized and 50% more profitable, and that firms specializing by industry alone grew slowest while those combining industry, role and service grew fastest ([Hinge 2017](https://hingemarketing.com/blog/story/2017-high-growth-research-report-released); [Hinge 2018](https://hingemarketing.com/?p=24417)). "We serve HOAs" is industry-only. The position has to name the role, the job and the proof.

The positioning statement, for the manager to read and think "that's exactly us": **"Small repairs for portfolio community managers: the $75 to $1,500 work orders contractors ignore, scheduled within 48 hours, photographed before and after, guaranteed by us (not by the handyman), and invoiced per association, under your approval cap, with COI, W-9 and additional-insured already on file."** Supporting proof points, in order: a published rate card (hourly, minimum, trip fee) because Mr. Handyman refuses to publish one; a one-page vendor packet with $1M/$2M GL, additional-insured endorsements for the association and the management company, workers' comp, W-9 and matching legal names; a 48-hour schedule commitment with written confirmation because responsiveness is the complaint most often lodged against managers themselves ([CT DCP](https://portal.ct.gov/dcp/knowledge-base/articles/consumer-hub/condos/most-common-hoa-complaints)); and per-work-order photo documentation framed as board and ombudsman protection, timely in a state where the new HOA ombudsman fields about one request a day and is running a 2026 listening tour on rising costs ([KSL](https://www.ksl.com:443/article/news/features/family/utah-hoa-ombudsman-launches-working-group-to-tackle-issues-and-rising-costs/51496526)). Words to ban: partner, one-stop, trusted, "licensed and insured" as a headline, "like our own." ## Conclusion

The research reframes the business. Tyler's asset is not a handyman network and not a 20% fee; it is the willingness to own the $200 job, the paperwork and the guarantee that every other local vendor treats as beneath them, sold to a few dozen overloaded portfolio managers who can each hand him 10 to 200 associations. That is a real niche with an empty position in Utah, and it is reachable in full for under $2,000 of trade-show and membership spend. It also comes with the two constraints Tyler has not priced: slow association payment that forces him to finance float, and a per-job coordination fee that cannot cover dispatch, insurance and a guarantee unless the labor spread does the heavy lifting.

The forward signal to watch is the two Utah regulatory clocks. The HOA registry's mandatory annual renewal will produce the first reliable, searchable count of associations and their managers through 2026 and 2027, and the January 2027 rental PM license should consolidate doors into licensed firms. The first makes the HOA prospect list better every quarter; the second enlarges the PM tier Tyler should enter in month four. Build one back office that satisfies Vantaca and AppFolio alike, run two campaigns that never use the same sentence, and treat resident TV mounts as the thank-you note at the end of a manager relationship rather than the reason for it.
