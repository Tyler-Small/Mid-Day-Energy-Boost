# Utah HOA / Community Association Management Market as a Buyer of Handyman and Small-Repair Services (as of Oct 2026)

Research method note: Findings below come from web-search result summaries (roughly 45 queries). Full-page fetches were not possible in this environment (DNS/proxy blocked every fetch, including caionline.org PDFs), and the search budget was exhausted before every sub-question could be covered. Each figure is therefore taken from a search snippet of the cited page, not from a full read; confidence levels reflect that. Items marked GAP were not found and should be verified in a follow-up pass (ideally by opening the CAI Utah legislative report PDFs and the FCAR Management Benchmarking PDF directly).

## Key Question 1: How many HOAs exist in Utah, how many are professionally managed, and how many units/households live in them?

### Takeaway
The best current figure is "more than 3,650 community associations" housing ~208,800 homes and ~644,000 Utahns (CAI Utah LAC, 2025 and 2026 reports); the ~3,400 figure was not found in any 2024-2026 source and is likely an older or FCAR-range estimate. No Utah-specific split of professionally managed vs. self-managed was found.

### Cited Findings
- CAI's Utah Legislative Action Committee end-of-session reports (2025 and 2026) state that approximately 644,000 Utahns live in 208,800 homes in more than 3,650 community associations. Confidence: medium-high (industry trade body, repeated across two years; figures are CAI estimates, not a registry count). — [CAI UT EOS Report 2025](https://www.caionline.org/getmedia/c73d664a-8616-40df-9722-0f53440e123a/UTEOSReport2025.pdf); [CAI 2026 Utah Dollar-a-Door form](https://www.caionline.org/getmedia/61514df7-209d-43b6-a924-a478ff5ece1f/2026-utah-dollar-a-doorform.pdf); [CAI UT EOS 2026](https://advocacy.caionline.org/uteos26/)
- CAI's 2024 Utah report said its advocacy covered "more than 3,570 community associations" during the 2024 session (so CAI's count rose ~80 from 2024 to 2025). Confidence: medium. — [CAI UT EOS Report 2024](https://dualauth.caionline.org/Advocacy/LAC/UT/Documents/UTEOSReport2024.pdf)
- Deseret News (Feb 3, 2025) reported 3,650 HOAs in Utah in coverage of the push for state oversight. Confidence: medium (press, likely citing CAI). — [Deseret News](https://www.deseret.com/utah/2025/02/03/hoa-headed-for-more-state-oversight/)
- KSL, citing a state records request, reported that 3,751 homeowner and condo associations had filed with the state registry since mid-June 2011; this is a cumulative filing count, not a current active count. Dept. of Commerce spokesperson Jennifer Bolton said registration is required but not enforced. Confidence: medium-high for the number as a cumulative figure; low as a count of active HOAs. — [KSL TV](https://ksltv.com/?p=393804)
- I found no source that gives "3,400" as Utah's HOA count; closest figures are 3,570 (2024) and 3,650 (2025-26). — [search across Deseret/KSL/CAI sources above]
- Utah's registry: the Department of Commerce maintains Utah's official registry of community and condominium associations; registration is mandatory with no exemptions; registrations must be renewed annually; a new registration system launched in September 2025 and the prior registry closed after October 31, 2025; the new registration fee is $90 (older guidance listed $37); associations not current cannot impose new liens or enforce existing liens. — [Utah HOA Registry FAQ](https://secure.utah.gov/hoa/overview/faq.html); [Commerce: Register Your HOA](https://commerce.utah.gov/hoa/new-registration/); [Commerce: All Utah HOAs Must Re-Register (Sept 12, 2025)](https://commerce.utah.gov/2025/09/12/all-utah-hoas-must-re-register-now-in-the-new-state-system/); [Registry overview](https://secure.utah.gov/hoa/overview/index.html)
- The Office of the Homeowners' Association Ombudsman was created by HB 217 (2025), effective May 7, 2025, and launched September 2025; its three main duties are maintaining the new HOA registry (with annual renewal), and issuing advisory opinions on questions of state law (not individual HOA rules). Public search tool: services.commerce.utah.gov/hoa; contact hoaoffice@utah.gov. — [Commerce news release Sept 8, 2025](https://commerce.utah.gov/2025/09/08/news-release-utah-department-of-commerce-launches-the-office-of-the-homeowners-association-ombudsman/); [Davis Journal](https://www.davisjournal.com/2025/09/15/546272/state-launches-new-office-to-oversee-hoas); [KJZZ](https://kjzz.com/news/eye-on-your-money/new-utah-office-established-to-oversee-hoa-communities)
- The ombudsman office was still issuing advisory opinions in 2026 (Advisory Opinion 2026-02 concerned a condominium association's registration of board contact information). — [Advisory Opinion 2026-02](https://commerce.utah.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Advisory-Opinion-2026-02.pdf)
- A new working group convened by the ombudsman office is spending much of 2026 on a statewide listening tour and could float policy changes for the 2027 legislative session. — [Hoodline, April 2026](https://hoodline.com/2026/04/fed-up-utah-homeowners-spur-state-hoa-fix-it-panel/)
- National context (label: NATIONAL): FCAR's 2025 Statistical Review puts the U.S. total at 373,000 associations and ~78.1 million residents in 2025 (2024: 369,000 communities, 28.8 million housing units, 77.1 million residents), with a 2026 estimate of 374,000-377,000. HOAs are 58-63% of the total, condominiums 35-40%, cooperatives 2-4%. Confidence: high. — [FCAR 2025 Statistical Review](https://foundation.caionline.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/2025StatisticalReviewFoundation.pdf); [FCAR 2023 Statistical Review](https://foundation.caionline.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/2023StatsReviewDigital-002.pdf)
- NATIONAL: Secondary sites citing the FCAR 2025 review report $124.2 billion in assessments collected and $31.1 billion contributed to reserves (about a quarter of assessments), and $13.1 trillion total home value. Confidence: medium (secondary citation of FCAR). — [HOPB HOA statistics](https://www.hopb.co/hoa-statistics)
- IBISWorld publishes a Utah-specific "Homeowners Associations" industry page (figures were not visible in search results). — [IBISWorld Utah HOAs](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/industry/utah/homeowners-associations/40889/)

### Inferences
- Using CAI's 208,800 homes / 3,650 associations gives an average of ~57 homes per Utah association, which implies a long tail of small associations (townhome/condo projects) that are unlikely to have in-house maintenance staff and are therefore natural buyers of outsourced handyman work.
- 644,000 residents is roughly 18-19% of Utah's ~3.5 million population, in line with the national share (78.1M of ~340M = ~23%).
- The cumulative 3,751 registrations vs. CAI's 3,650 estimate suggests the state registry and CAI's count are in the same range; the new mandatory annual-renewal registry (Sept 2025 onward) will produce the first reliable active count. The registry's public search (services.commerce.utah.gov/hoa) could be mined for management-company contact fields to build a prospect list.
- Daily enforcement via the lien penalty means HOAs that intend to collect assessments have a strong incentive to be registered, so the new registry should approach full coverage over 2026-2027.

### Gaps
- Professionally managed vs. self-managed split for Utah: not found. FCAR historically publishes a national split in its Statistical Review, but the search budget ran out before this could be retrieved (NATIONAL figure also unverified).
- County-level counts (Salt Lake, Utah, Davis) and city-level counts (Lehi, Provo, Saratoga Springs, Eagle Mountain, Daybreak/South Jordan): not found. The state registry search tool would be the primary source.
- Current active registration count in the new ombudsman registry (post Oct 31, 2025): not published in any result found.
- Origin of the "~3,400" figure: not located.

## Key Question 3: What do CAI and FCAR say nationally about manager workload, vendors per community, maintenance share of budget, manager turnover/burnout, and vendor-related pain points?

### Takeaway
National (NATIONAL label applies to this whole section): managers typically carry 8-10 communities, 71% report moderate-to-severe burnout and 80% have considered leaving, 97% of firms report a manager shortage, and common-area maintenance is typically the largest operating expense (roughly 20-35% of budget by secondary sources). No CAI figure on vendors per community was found.

### Cited Findings
- FCAR burnout survey (July 2026; 287 community association professionals; sponsored by Vote HOA Now, a conflict-of-interest note): 71% reported moderate to severe burnout; 80% have thought about leaving because of stress or emotional strain; top drivers were difficult homeowner interactions, board conflict, and heavy workload. ## Key Question 4: How do HOA managers pay vendors (approval thresholds, cycles, platforms) and what is the typical days-to-pay? Nonpayment/dispute data?

### Takeaway
Payment runs through manager-to-board-to-accounting approval chains, historically by paper check (over 70% of HOA vendor payments per AvidXchange), with board-signature requirements and monthly board meetings as the main delay sources; platforms (Vantaca/AvidXchange, CINC VendorPay) now offer ACH in 1-5 business days or checks in 7-10 business days once an invoice is approved. No published days-to-pay benchmark or nonpayment statistics were found.

### Cited Findings
- AvidXchange (via PYMNTS): more than 70% of property vendor payments in the HOA vertical are still made via paper check; HOAs that require board member signatures to approve payments "can see significant delays in paying vendors when they must collect paper signatures"; each HOA customer is its own legal entity with its own invoice requirements. Date of article uncertain (possibly 2018-2019; treat as older). Confidence: medium. — [PYMNTS: AvidXchange HOA payables](https://www.pymnts.com/?p=505633); [PYMNTS 2018 AvidXchange HOA vertical](https://www.pymnts.com/startup-check-in/2018/avidxchange-hoa-vertical-ap-automation-payment-methods/)
- Vantaca Vendor Pay (vendor-stated timelines, after invoices clear Vantaca's internal AP approval workflow): virtual credit card for instant payment; same-day ACH in 1 business day; standard ACH in 3-5 business days; check in 7-10 business days. Vendors choose method and timing. — [Vantaca FAQ: vendor portal](https://www.vantaca.com/vantaca-faq/which-community-association-management-software-has-the-best-vendor-portal)
- Vantaca Invoice Pay is powered by AvidXchange and covers invoice capture, approvals, and vendor payments; Vantaca sends one consolidated remittance email per payment batch listing all invoices paid to the vendor from the previous day. — [Vantaca payment solutions](https://www.vantaca.com/hoa-payment-solutions-vantaca); [Vantaca support: remittance](https://support.vantaca.com/hc/en-us/articles/360029645331); [Vantaca Vendor Pay terms](https://www.vantaca.com/vantaca-vendor-pay-terms)
- CINC Systems offers "CINC VendorPay powered by AvidXchange" for HOA AP automation. — [AvidXchange CINC demo page](https://automate.avidxchange.com/CINC-HOA-Demo-Request.html)
- Bank/payment providers in the HOA vertical: First Citizens Vendor Pay (approval workflows mirroring existing processes; virtual card, ACH, check; cash back on virtual card payments); REPAY (manager approves invoice, REPAY pays via vendor's preferred method). — [First Citizens community association payments](https://www.firstcitizens.com/commercial/solutions/community-association-banking/payment-and-technology-solutions); [REPAY HOA management](https://repay.com/who-we-serve/hoa-management/)
- Approval thresholds are set per association in governing documents: some associations require two signatures on checks over $1,000, others set the limit at $5,000; whoever approves invoices should not prepare or sign checks. — [BiggerPockets: preventing theft of HOA funds](https://www.biggerpockets.com/member-blogs/3963/47355-preventing-theft-of-your-hoa-funds)
- Boards should check CC&Rs, bylaws and state statutes for expenditure thresholds that may require member consent; "many CC&Rs and bylaws specify approval thresholds." — [Ward and Smith: HOA vendor agreements](https://www.wardandsmith.com/article/contract-smarter-not-harder-best-practices-for-entering-into-and-managing-hoa-vendor-agreements); [Vivo HOA board guide](https://a.storyblok.com/f/291606774941774/x/9bb6adbbb0/vivo_control_hoa_board_guide_en.pdf)
- Anti-fraud guidance recommends that all large and ongoing vendor services be committed to a signed contract with full board approval before a vendor receives payment. — [CRI Advisors: 10 anti-fraud recommendations](https://www.criadv.com/insight/community-association-recommendations/)
- AP-software vendors describe the typical chain as manager -> board -> accounting, with payment "delayed for several days between each stage," and warn that "a simple bill approval can take several weeks" when the manager is slow; payment release stays with two authorized signers (usually treasurer and president) approving in the invoice queue, so if signers act only at monthly meetings, payments wait for that cycle. Confidence: low-medium (vendor marketing). — [LeapAP condo/HOA AP template](https://www.leapap.com/blog/condo-hoa-accounts-payable-template); [LeapAP help](https://help.leapap.com/management-company-or-condo-hoa-board); [Invoice Data Extraction: HOA AP workflow](https://invoicedataextraction.com/blog/hoa-accounts-payable)
- Clark Simson Miller (which also markets in Ogden, UT) describes online invoice approval where the board gets an instant notification that an invoice needs approval, and some vendors are offered electronic payment. — [CSM bill and invoice payments](https://clarksimsonmiller.com/bill-and-invoice-payments/)
- Anecdotal complaints: a Minnesota HOA working-group presentation listed "slow payments to smaller vendors" and payments made without board approval among complaints about management companies; a Reddit commenter (snapshot, March 2026) claimed a Florida management company "never pays vendors on time, if at all" (single anecdote). — [MN Legislature: Kathleen Phillips presentation, Dec 2024](https://www.lcc.mn.gov/cichoa/meetings/20241206/Kathleen-Phillips-Powerpoint); [Reddit snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1rrrc94/snapshots/2026-03-13T06%3A26%3A00.56373Z)
- Nevada regulators check whether two board members review and approve invoices and examine electronic-signature payment controls (illustrates the two-approver norm; Nevada-specific, not Utah). — [Nevada RED memo](https://red.nv.gov/uploadedFiles/rednvgov/Content/Meetings/CIC/2024/Supporting_Materials/December/Clearacre-Condominium-HOA_2024-137_Supplemental-Memorandum.pdf)

### Inferences
- Realistic days-to-pay for a small Utah vendor: invoice approval (days to weeks, depending on whether the amount is under the manager's authority or needs board sign-off at a monthly meeting) plus 1-10 business days for disbursement. A vendor that keeps individual invoices under common board-approval thresholds ($1,000-$5,000) and enrolls in ACH/virtual card via AvidXchange/Vantaca will be paid fastest.
- Because each association is a separate legal entity, a handyman serving 30 associations under one management company may face 30 separate approval queues and remittances; enrolling once in the management company's AP platform (vendor portal, W-9, COI on file) is the practical way to collapse this friction.
- Payment reliability is structurally decent (assessment-funded entities, audited books, two-signer controls) but slow; disputes are more likely about scope/approval than solvency.

## Key Question 5: What do HOA managers say they want from a "go-to" maintenance vendor? Quotes from forums and trade articles.

### Takeaway
The consistent, documented asks are: current certificate of insurance ($1M per occurrence / $2M aggregate general liability) naming the association and management company as additional insured, workers' compensation proof, a W-9 before first payment, active licensing, responsiveness, and clear communication; insurance is expected "even for handyman type jobs." Reddit/LinkedIn CAM quotes were largely not retrievable (search results returned industry pages instead).

### Cited Findings
- HOAMCO vendor requirements: every vendor must provide a current liability certificate, workers' compensation proof, and a completed W-9; certificate minimums of $1M per occurrence and $2M aggregate with the association added as additional insured. — [HOAMCO vendor services](https://hoamco.com/vendor-services/)

## Key Question 6: Utah-specific: Community Association Act reserves/maintenance requirements, CAI Utah chapter size, vendor directories and trade shows

### Takeaway
Utah Code 57-8a-211 requires a reserve analysis (secondary sources: full analysis at least every 6 years, update every 3, annual summary to owners, reserve line item in budget) but sets no funding percentage; the CAI Utah Chapter has about 320 members and ~$105k revenue (2024); no Utah CAI trade show or Utah HOA vendor directory was found, though BOMA Utah holds an annual trade show.

### Cited Findings
- Utah Code 57-8a-211 (current version effective May 5, 2021) defines "reserve analysis" as an analysis to determine the need for a reserve fund and its appropriate amount, and "reserve funds" as money for repairing or replacing common areas with a useful life of three years or more and less than 30 years remaining, where the general budget cannot reasonably cover the cost. — [Utah Code 57-8a-211](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title57/Chapter8A/C57-8a-S211_2021050520210505.html)
- Secondary summaries: associations must conduct a full reserve analysis at least every six years, review and update it at least every three years, provide an annual summary to owners, include a reserve fund line item in the annual budget, and Utah sets no required funding percentage or dollar amount. — [PropFusion Utah reserve study guide](https://www.propfusion.com/law-guide/utah-reserve-study-requirements); [CommunityPay Utah laws](https://www.communitypay.us/laws/utah/)
- Legislative history: S.B. 278 (2010) first enacted the reserve analysis section with a five-year minimum interval; S.B. 64 (2013) added the yearly summary to members, full copy on request, and the budget line item. — [S.B. 278 (2010)](https://le.utah.gov/~2010/bills/sbillenr/SB0278.pdf); [S.B. 64 (2013)](https://le.utah.gov/~2013/bills/sbillenr/sb0064.pdf)
- The Utah Community Association Act is Title 57, Chapter 8a (HOAs); condominiums are under Title 57, Chapter 8. — [HOPB: Utah Community Association Act](https://www.hopb.co/utah-community-association-act-title-57-chapter-8a); [CommunityPay: Utah Code 57-8-7.5](https://www.communitypay.us/laws/utah/statute/utah-code-57-8-7-5/)
