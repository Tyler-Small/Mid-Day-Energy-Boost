## Q1. Are HOA/CAM and rental PM different professions? (associations, licenses, software, overlap)

### Takeaway
They are distinct professions with separate trade bodies (CAI vs NARPM/IREM/NAA), separate credential ladders (CMCA/AMS/PCAM vs RMP/MPM/CRMC, CPM, CAM), different software stacks, and in Utah different licensing treatment (rental PM requires a real-estate or new property-manager license; HOA management is exempt). A minority of Utah firms do both, but the large national HOA firms (FirstService, Spectrum, Clark Simson Miller) do not manage rentals.

### Cited Findings
- CAI explicitly distinguishes the roles: community association managers work for the owners' collective body (budgets, board reports, covenant enforcement, authorizing association payments), while property managers "focus on individual units" (tenants, rent, complaints). A CAM is hired by a vote of the board; a PM is hired by the individual owner. — [CAI HOA Resources: distinct roles](https://hoaresources.caionline.org/how-community-managers-and-property-managers-have-distinct-roles/); [NEREJ: Community assn managers vs property managers](https://beta.nerej.com/community-assn-managers-vs-property-managers-by-john-ganoe) (HIGH)
- CAI says state legislatures "occasionally have sought, inappropriately, to license community association managers as real estate brokers or property managers" — i.e., CAI actively lobbies to keep the professions legally separate. — [CAI press room: The Profession of Community Association Manager](https://www.caionline.org/press-room/the-profession-of-community-association-manager/) (HIGH)
- CAI credential ladder: CMCA (entry) -> AMS -> PCAM (top). CAI has 50,000+ members and 64 chapters. — [CAI 50,000 members release](https://caionline.org/community-associations-institute-reaches-50-000-members); [FirstService: certifications](https://www.fsresidential.com/corporate/news-and-articles/articles/why-property-management-certifications-matter/) (HIGH)
- NARPM: ~6,000 members (Oct 2024 president interview), focused on single-family/small residential rental management; moving toward a single "one class type" membership for firms and support staff. — [Business View Magazine, Oct 2024](https://businessviewmagazine.com/brochures/oct-2024/narpm/3); [Second Nature blog, Jul 2025](https://www.secondnature.com/blog/best-property-management-associations) (MED)
- CAI 2025 Statistical Review: ~60,000-65,000 community association managers; ~9,000-10,000 CAM management companies; ~373,000 associations housing 78.1M residents in 29.6M units; 30-40% of associations are self-managed. — [CAI Foundation 2025 Statistical Review (PDF)](https://foundation.caionline.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/2025StatisticalReviewFoundation.pdf) (HIGH)
- Utah licensing, rental PM: Utah Code 61-2f requires that "unless an individual is licensed as a principal broker, associate broker, sales agent, or property manager, an individual may not, for consideration, engage in property management," subject to exemptions in 61-2f-202.5. — [Utah Code 61-2f Part 2 (PDF)](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title61/Chapter2F/C61-2f-P2_1800010118000101.pdf) (HIGH)
- Utah HB 337 (2025 GS) created a stand-alone "property manager" license for managers who do not do sales; HB 1002 (2025 First Special Session) clarified that broker/sales-agent licensees may still manage property and moved the effective date to July 1, 2026. — [HB 337 enrolled (PDF)](https://le.utah.gov/Session/2025/bills/enrolled/HB0337.pdf); [HB 1002 (PDF)](https://le.utah.gov/Session/2025S1/bills/introduced/HB1002.pdf); [Duckworth Legal Group](https://duckworthlegalgroup.com/utah-property-manager-licensing-hb337/) (HIGH)
- Utah Division of Real Estate's property-management page states HB 377 (2026 GS) extended implementation of the property-manager license to January 1, 2027; a bill tracker says HB 377 also narrowed the license to residential rental management. One summary gives a May 6, 2026 effective date, conflicting with Jan 1, 2027. — [Utah DRE Property Management page](https://commerce.utah.gov/realestate/property-management/); [Better Utah Institute tracker HB 377](https://billtracker.betterutahinstitute.org/bills/HB0377); conflicting: [PolicyRisk HB 377](https://policyrisk.com/state-bill/UT-HB377-2026GS) (MED; dates conflict — verify)
- Utah DRE Q4-2025 newsletter: the administrative rule for the new license was still being drafted; the application would be available on the effective date. Proposed rule R162-2i addresses residential property management licensing. — [DRE 2025 Q4 newsletter](https://commerce.utah.gov/realestate/newsletters/2025-q4/); [Utah Rules bulletin DAR 40045](https://rules.utah.gov/publicat/bulletin/2016/20160115/40045.htm) (MED)
- Utah licensing, HOA managers: CAI's Utah 2025 end-of-session report says the final version of the bill "specifically exempts common interest association managers from the property manager licensing requirement," along with assistants and bookkeepers. A Rental Housing Association of Utah paper states that under prior law, self-managed HOAs and companies managing HOAs were already exempt. No Utah broker license is required for a community/condo association manager. — [CAI Utah EOS Report 2025 (PDF)](https://www.caionline.org/getmedia/c73d664a-8616-40df-9722-0f53440e123a/UTEOSReport2025.pdf); [RHA Utah: Property Management Licensing (DOCX)](https://assets.noviams.com/novi-file-uploads/uaa/Property_Management_Licensing-10008239.docx); [AllPropertyManagement: Utah laws](https://www.allpropertymanagement.com/resources/property-management-laws/utah) (HIGH)
- Utah HOAs must register with the Dept. of Commerce HOA registry ($90 fee); all associations had to re-register in a new system by Oct 31, 2025; unregistered associations cannot impose or enforce liens. No exemptions. — [Utah Commerce: re-register notice, Sep 2025](https://commerce.utah.gov/2025/09/12/all-utah-hoas-must-re-register-now-in-the-new-state-system/); [Utah HOA registry FAQ](https://secure.utah.gov/hoa/overview/faq.html) (HIGH)
- Software: Vantaca (HOA-only) claims 400+ management companies serving 5M+ homes; G2 segment mix shows Vantaca 58% small-business / 40% mid-market vs AppFolio 86% small-business / 12% mid-market. No independent market-share data found for Vantaca/CINC vs AppFolio/Buildium/Yardi/RentManager. — [GetApp Vantaca alternatives](https://www.getapp.co.uk/alternatives/2050285/vantaca); [G2 Community Association Management category](https://g2crowd.com/categories/community-association-management) (LOW-MED)
- Overlap in Utah: BiggerPockets directory profiles list Maxfield Property Management (claims to be Utah's only CRMC firm), Wasatch Leasing & Property Management, and Vision Real Estate Property Management as offering both HOA/association and long-term rental management. FirstService Residential (Salt Lake, Provo, St. George), Spectrum AM (Provo) and Clark Simson Miller (Provo) are HOA-only. — [BiggerPockets Provo PMs](https://www.biggerpockets.com/ut/provo/property-managers?page=1); [BiggerPockets SLC PMs p2](https://www.biggerpockets.com/ut/salt-lake-city/property-managers?page=2); [FirstService Utah](https://www.fsresidential.com/utah/utah-local-services/); [Spectrum Provo](https://spectrumam.com/utah/provo-hoa-management/); [CSM Provo](https://clarksimsonmiller.com/provo-ut-hoa-management/) (MED; self-reported)
- NARPM Utah chapter is housed inside the Rental Housing Association of Utah (rhautah.org/narpm, 801-487-5619); manager dues $100/$300, affiliate (vendor) dues $300/yr; monthly meetings; annual November education conference & trade show. Affiliates get a NARPM directory listing. — [NARPM Utah supplier application (PDF)](https://assets.noviams.com/novi-file-uploads/uaa/NARPM/SUPPLIERS_NARPM_UTAH_CHAPTER_Application.pdf); [NARPM Utah manager application (PDF)](https://assets.noviams.com/novi-file-uploads/uaa/NARPM/NARPM_MANAGER_MEMBER_Application.pdf) (HIGH)

### Inferences
- Because Utah is layering a new rental-PM license (2026-27) while explicitly exempting HOA managers, the legal/regulatory moat between the two professions in Utah is widening, not narrowing. Expect fewer "we do both" firms over time and more specialization.
- The NARPM-Utah/RHA co-location means one $300 affiliate membership reaches both single-family PMs (NARPM) and multifamily operators (RHA) in one organization — a cheaper go-to-market than CAI Utah for a vendor.
- HOA-focused software (Vantaca, CINC) skews to larger mid-market firms; rental-PM software skews small-business, consistent with a more fragmented rental PM buyer base.

### Gaps
- Could not find CAI Utah chapter membership counts or a count of registered Utah HOAs (the Commerce registry has no public total; contact hoaoffice@utah.gov).
- Exact statutory text of the HOA-manager exemption in HB 337/HB 377 not read (fetch blocked); CAI summary and RHA paper are consistent but secondary.
- HB 377 effective-date conflict (Jan 1, 2027 per DRE page vs May 6, 2026 per PolicyRisk) unresolved.
- No survey data on what share of US or Utah firms manage both HOAs and rentals.

## Q2. Rental PM maintenance economics: in-house vs outsourced, markups, work-order volume, ticket size, response speed

### Takeaway
No NARPM/Buildium survey publishes a clean in-house-vs-vendor split; the consistent industry picture is a hybrid, with vendors handling a growing share (Property Meld: 56.9% of plumbing work orders went to vendors in Oct 2025, up from 34.5% a year earlier). Markups of 10-20% on vendor invoices (or $20-$100 flat per work order) are the norm; roughly 2 work orders/unit/year at ~$200-$500 per ticket; ~$1,700-$2,600 annual maintenance spend per unit; and a 3-7 day repair clock is the operating benchmark, with Utah's habitability statute imposing a 3-calendar-day corrective period.

### Cited Findings
- In-house vs vendor: Property Meld Oct 2025 benchmarking — 56.9% more plumbing work orders were scheduled with vendors than in-house techs, vs 34.5% a year earlier (platform data, not survey). — [Property Meld PM25 Oct benchmarking (PDF)](https://propertymeld.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/PM25-Industry-Benchmarking-Report-October-FNL.pdf) (MED)
- Buildium 2027 industry report (survey of ~1,000 US PM professionals, May 2026): 11% of companies plan to bring third-party services in-house this year; maintenance named a top challenge by 32% of companies in 2026, up from 24% in 2025. — [Buildium 2027 Property Management Industry Report](https://www.buildium.com/resource/2027-property-management-industry-report/) (MED; gated, from extract)
- Buildium 2026 report: 38% of rental owners name maintenance their top stressor; 93% of companies saw expenses rise, led by vendor/contractor labor (70%) and materials (64%). — [Buildium: maintenance as competitive advantage, 2026](https://www.buildium.com/blog/how-property-managers-can-turn-maintenance-into-a-competitive-advantage/); [Buildium 2026 Industry Report](https://www.buildium.com/resource/2026-property-management-industry-report/) (MED)
- Buildium guidance: in-house staff for daily tasks, vendors for larger/specialized jobs; in-house makes sense only when the portfolio keeps them busy full-time. — [Buildium: property maintenance services list](https://www.buildium.com/blog/property-maintenance-services-list/); [Buildium multifamily maintenance guide](https://www.buildium.com/blog/multifamily-maintenance-guide/) (MED)
- Vendor blogs claim a typical hybrid of 70-80% of work-order volume in-house / 20-30% outsourced, with no survey cited. — [OxMaint: outsourcing vs in-house](https://oxmaint.com/industries/property-management/property-maintenance-outsourcing-vs-in-house-cost-risk-comparison) (LOW)
- Markups: "Standard range 10% to 20% markup on maintenance and repair costs"; some firms use a flat $20-$100 per work order; other guides cite 10-15% or $25-$50 flat coordination fees. Owners split on whether this is fair or "double-charging." — [Bluefield Group fee guide](https://www.bluefieldgroup.com/blog/a-transparent-guide-to-property-management-fees-what-landlords-should-expect/); [Tellus fee guide](https://resources.tellusapp.com/passive-income/property-management/property-management-fees); [Reedy & Co rates](https://www.reedyandcompany.com/blog/how-much-do-property-management-companies-charge-rates-explained/); [HelloData commercial fees](https://www.hellodata.ai/help-articles/what-are-typical-commercial-property-management-fees) (MED; multiple consistent secondary sources; no NARPM survey found)
- Practitioner debate: a BiggerPockets thread "Property Manager Charges 10% markup for all service calls" includes a PM arguing they have never charged a markup in decades and that the markup model is a conflict of interest; an investor thread worries about $35-$100 trip charges eating profits; a PM-authored thread complains a $3,500 water-heater quote should cost $1,600 depending on "who's doing" it. — [BP: 10% markup thread](https://www.biggerpockets.com/posts/7252173); [BP: repair quotes seem high](https://www.biggerpockets.com/forums/600/topics/612814-repair-quotes-by-property-manager-seem-extremely-high?page=1); [BP: $3,500 water heater](https://www.biggerpockets.com/forums/888/topics/1285072-a-3-500-water-heater-replacement-should-cost-1-600-the-difference-is-whos-doing) (MED; anecdotal)
- Work orders per unit: Bay Property Management Group 2025 dataset — ~2.21 work orders/unit/year, average repair ~$494 (single firm). Property Meld's July 2025 report has a section "What's behind the drop in work orders per unit?" but the figure was not extractable. — [Property Meld PM25 July (PDF)](https://propertymeld.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/PM25-Industry-Benchmarking-Report-July-v3.pdf); Bay PMG figure via [Lula maintenance cost guide](https://lula.life/articles/rental-property-maintenance-costs-guide) (LOW-MED)
- Ticket size: Property Meld Oct 2024 report lists average repair cost ~$218.50 per repair (text extraction jumbled); Lessen 2024 (680,000+ work orders, $470M) puts median cost per work order at $327-$707. — [Property Meld PM24 Oct (PDF)](https://propertymeld.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/PM24-Industry-Benchmarking-Report-October-v4-FNL-opt.pdf); Lessen via [Bond benchmark post](https://www.bondknows.com/blog/property-maintenance-cost-benchmark) (LOW-MED)
- Annual spend per unit: Property Meld target $1,680-$2,592/unit/yr, <12% of rent roll; 2025 annual report: spend per unit down 2.6% vs 2024 while vendor invoice amounts rose 4.375%; May 2026 monthly spend $250.16 (unit basis unclear), +4.7% YoY. — [Property Meld homepage](https://propertymeld.com/); [PM25 Annual Benchmarking 2025 (PDF)](https://propertymeld.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/PM25-Annual-Benchmarking-Report-2025.pdf); [PM26 benchmarking (PDF)](https://propertymeld.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/PM26-Industry-Benchmarking-Report-FNL.pdf) (MED)
- Speed of repair (submission to invoice): Property Meld target 3.4-6.8 days; 2025 operator average 6.9 days; "world class" 2.7 days; 6.8 days is the tipping point where resident satisfaction declines; properties using the same three vendors for a major category averaged 8.2 days. — [Property Meld: vendor management 2025](https://propertymeld.com/blog/what-we-learned-in-vendor-management-in-2025/); [Property Meld benchmarks blog](https://propertymeld.com/blog/property-maintenance-benchmarks-to-monitor-efficiency/) (MED)
- HappyCo platform benchmark: 66% of work orders completed within 24 hours, average 3.88 days. — [HappyCo: Maintenance Metric Trap](https://happy.co/resources/the-maintenance-metric-trap) (MED)
- Renter expectations: Buildium 2026 renters survey (n=1,500) — 84% expect a PM response within 24 hours; "better follow-through on maintenance requests" is the #1 requested improvement (39%); maintenance is the area where renters are most open to automation. — [Buildium 2026 Renters Report](https://www.buildium.com/resource/2026-renters-report/) (MED)
- Utah legal clock: Utah Code 57-22-6 (Fit Premises Act, current version eff. July 1, 2024) defines the "corrective period" as 3 calendar days for a habitability standard and 10 calendar days for a rental-agreement requirement, running from the renter's written notice; a "deficient condition" excludes damage caused by the renter, family, guests, or illegal use. — [Utah Code 57-22-6](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title57/Chapter22/C57-22-S6_2023050320240701.html); [Justia 57-22-6 (2020)](https://law.justia.com/codes/utah/2020/title-57/chapter-22/section-6/) (HIGH)
- Maintenance-coordination-as-a-service pricing: Latchel via RentRedi — $15/unit/month (24/7 emergency only) to $25/unit/month (full coordination), month-to-month; 2019 historical pricing was $25 account fee + $0.80/unit/mo for after-hours. Lula — no monthly or setup fee, flat-rate per-job catalog, earns on completed work. AppFolio integrates the Lula vendor network. — [RentRedi/Latchel](https://rentredi.com/?p=5279); [TechCrunch 2019 Latchel](https://techcrunch.com/2019/02/22/latchel-wants-to-make-maintenance-easier-for-landlords-and-property-managers); [Lula Core](https://lula.life/core); [AppFolio: Lula vendor network](https://www.appfolio.com/articles/lula-vendor-network) (MED)
- Portfolio size: NARPM estimated the average PM has ~300 units under management (~200 owner-clients) — 2018, dated. NARPM Portland chapter: 19 firms, 5,000+ units (~260/firm, my arithmetic). — [Think Realty: managing large portfolios](https://thinkrealty.com/article/managing-large-portfolios-of-properties/); [NARPM PDX chapter](https://narpmpdx.starchapter.com/about) (LOW; dated)

### Inferences
- A 300-door NARPM-type firm at ~2.2 work orders/unit/yr generates ~650 tickets/yr (~55/month) at a few hundred dollars each — a steady, repeatable flow a handyman coordinator can plan staffing around, unlike lumpy HOA common-area projects.
- The 10-20% markup norm is the direct comparable for a third-party coordinator's fee; PMs will tolerate paying a coordinator only if it does not stack on top of their own markup (i.e., the coordinator must replace, not add, a layer) or if it buys speed that keeps them under the ~6.8-day satisfaction threshold and Utah's 3-day habitability clock.
- Rising vendor labor costs (70% of firms) plus the 2025 slip in repair speed to 6.9 days signal that vendor capacity, not demand, is the binding constraint — favorable for a coordinator who can source trades.

### Gaps
- No NARPM-published survey on in-house vs outsourced share or markup prevalence was found; the 10-20% figure rests on secondary fee guides.
- Property Meld's work-orders-per-unit figure and the full 2025 vendor-management findings were not extractable (fetch blocked).
- No Utah-specific maintenance KPI or markup data found.
- AppFolio 2025 Benchmark Report work-order completion figure not surfaced.

## Q3. Who decides and who pays the vendor; payment timing and reliability

### Takeaway
Rental PM: the PM decides within a contractually set spend limit (owner approval above it), pays the vendor from the owner's trust/maintenance reserve, and bills tenants (or deducts deposits) for tenant-caused damage — payment is fast if the reserve is funded. HOA: the manager can approve only small/emergency items; most spend requires board approval (often at a meeting), payment comes from association funds via a manager-to-board-to-accounting chain, 70%+ of HOA vendor payments are still paper checks, and approval chains can stretch to weeks.

### Cited Findings
- Rental PM payment: "repair costs are usually paid using the property owner's money, often through a maintenance reserve account managed by the property management company"; tenants pay when the issue is caused by their actions/negligence; managers document, coordinate, and bill the responsible party. — [FirstService NC: Do property managers pay for repairs](https://www.fsresidential.com/north-carolina/news-events/articles/do-property-managers-pay-for-repairs/); [Bay Mgmt Group: Do PMs pay for repairs](https://www.baymgmtgroup.com/blog/do-property-managers-pay-for-repairs-heres-the-truth/) (MED)
- Deposit mechanics: PM accounting routinely includes "withholdings from security deposits to pay for damage to rental units" and pass-through of charges (e.g., carpet cleaning) to tenants. — [AccountingTools: Property Management Accounting podcast](https://www.accountingtools.com/podcast-blog/258) (MED)
- Owner due-diligence guidance: ask the PM how quickly it pays vendors and whether vendors wait "until rent is collected or a unit rents"; long waits strain vendor relationships and push vendors to raise prices; a properly funded reserve prevents this. — [BiggerPockets: Handling tenant and owner funds](https://www.biggerpockets.com/member-blogs/769/blog_posts/3809-handling-tenant-and-owner-funds); [ManageMyProperty: Handling funds](https://www.managemyproperty.com/articles/handling-tenant-and-owner-funds-11) (MED)
- Maintenance reserve/threshold concept (PM holds owner reserve; spends above threshold need owner approval). — [TouchPoint PM: Maintenance Reserve and Threshold](https://touchpointpm.notion.site/Maintenance-Reserve-and-Threshold-b41758fe64464bcea8289d46f8bd6131); [Utopia Management owner FAQ](https://utopiamanagement.com/resources/owner-faq) (MED)
- Utah statute reinforces the tenant-pays rule: a "deficient condition" under 57-22-6 excludes damage caused by the renter or guests, so landlords/PMs can charge tenants for it. — [Utah Code 57-22-6](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title57/Chapter22/C57-22-S6_2023050320240701.html) (HIGH)
- HOA decision authority: the board sets the budget and approves vendor contracts, often delegating day-to-day operations to the management company, which hires/supervises landscapers, janitorial and repair technicians. — [FirstService MN: common area maintenance](https://www.fsresidential.com/minnesota/news-events/articles/demystifying-maintenance-and-repair-roles-for-resi/) (MED)
- HOA manager spend cap: management agreements typically let the manager approve maintenance "up to $[Amount] without board approval" and authorize emergency repairs with after-the-fact notice; many associations require two signatures on reserve checks and a board co-signature above a threshold on operating checks. Caps are set by the contract/bylaws, not statute; an Arizona case shows a bylaw spending cap raised from $50 to $7,500 by member vote. — [AI Lawyer HOA mgmt agreement template](https://ailawyer.pro/templates/hoa-property-management-agreement-template); [LegalClarity: HOA PM duties](https://legalclarity.org/what-are-hoa-property-manager-duties-and-responsibilities/); [LegalClarity: board spending limits](https://legalclarity.org/what-are-an-hoa-boards-spending-limits/); [AZ HOA Watch: Mullane v. Thunder FE](https://azhoawatch.org/?p=36229) (MED)
- HOA payment reliability: "In the HOA vertical, more than 70 percent of property vendor payments are still made via paper check"; HOAs requiring board signatures "can see significant delays in paying vendors," including when one board member travels. — [PYMNTS: AvidXchange on HOA payables](https://www.pymnts.com/?p=505633) (MED)
- HOA approval chain: manager forwards invoice to board, board responds, accounting pays; "payment can be delayed for several days between each stage," and if the manager overlooks the email "a simple bill approval can take several weeks" with late fees. — [Clark Simson Miller: HOA bill and invoice payments](https://clarksimsonmiller.com/bill-and-invoice-payments/) (MED; vendor marketing)
- HOA AP requires each invoice to show approver, property/service, operating vs reserve coding, and which association in the portfolio it belongs to; incomplete invoices stall. Vendors are often asked to invoice each community by name with separate billing profiles. — [InvoiceDataExtraction: HOA accounts payable](https://invoicedataextraction.com/blog/hoa-accounts-payable); [LeapAP: Condo/HOA AP template](https://www.leapap.com/blog/condo-hoa-accounts-payable-template) (MED)
- HOA vendor onboarding: e.g., HOAMCO requires a current COI with $1M per occurrence / $2M aggregate general liability, HOAMCO and all its associations named additional insured, proof of workers' comp, and a completed W-9 before first payment. Law-firm guidance recommends COIs, cancellation notice, and additional-insured status. — [HOAMCO vendor services](https://hoamco.com/vendor-services/); [Ward and Smith: HOA vendor agreements](https://www.wardandsmith.com/article/contract-smarter-not-harder-best-practices-for-entering-into-and-managing-hoa-vendor-agreements); [RealManage: service contract essentials](https://blog.realmanage.com/en-us/5-essentials-for-any-service-contract-at-your-hoa) (MED)
- Contractor-side reality: "due in 30 days does not always mean you'll actually receive payment within 30 days." — [Wexford Insurance: net-30 slow payers](https://www.wexfordins.com/post/net-30-slow-payers-contractors) (LOW; general)

### Inferences
- Decision latency is structurally shorter for rental PMs (one manager, a dollar threshold, a tenant clock) than for HOAs (manager cap, then board cycle). A handyman coordinator should expect same-day/next-day authorization from PMs for sub-threshold work, but days-to-weeks for anything over an HOA manager's cap.
- Payment reliability for a rental PM depends on reserve funding and PM discipline; for an HOA it depends on board responsiveness and paper-check processes. Neither is uniformly fast; HOAs are more process-bound.
- A coordinator serving HOAs must carry $1M/$2M GL, workers' comp, and additional-insured endorsements per association — a fixed cost that favors multi-association management-company relationships over single boards.

### Gaps
- No quantitative benchmark (days-to-pay) found for either HOA or rental PM vendor payments.
- Typical rental-PM owner-approval thresholds ($300/$500 etc.) could not be sourced before the search budget ran out.
- Utah-specific HOA board meeting cadence / approval practices not sourced.

## Q4. Large apartment communities (200+ units): in-house vs outsourced, staffing, where they outsource

### Takeaway
Institutional multifamily runs on-site maintenance teams at roughly 1 tech per 100 units (NAA's long-standing rule, 2020), outsourcing licensed/specialty trades (HVAC, elevator, fire systems), surge turnover work, and increasingly "centralized" or vendor-network overflow because maintenance techs are the hardest role to hire and retain.

### Cited Findings
- NAA staffing best-practices (2020): "the general rule has always been one full-time office staff and one full-time maintenance staff for every 100 units," noting the rule "has evolved." — [NAA staffing best practices (PDF)](https://www.naahq.org/sites/default/files/naa_bestpractices_satffing.pdf) (MED; dated 2020)
- UpKeep guidance: 2 techs per 100 units for a new complex plus a maintenance manager; alternatively 1 tech per 150-200 units plus manager; "some get away with one for every 500"; under 100 units typically a single maintenance manager. — [UpKeep: technicians per apartment unit](https://upkeep.com/learning/technicians-per-apartment-unit/) (LOW-MED; vendor)
- Turnover: NAA 2019 survey average resident turnover 46.8%; ~$1,800 per move-out = $168,000+/yr for a 200-unit community; 2021 turnover 46.9% (via Lula). — [NAA Apartment Turnover 2025 (PDF)](https://naahq.org/sites/default/files/2025-03/01a.%20Apartment%20Turnover%202025.pdf); [Lula: cut apartment turnover time](https://lula.life/articles/best-ways-to-cut-down-on-apartment-turnover-time) (MED)
- Some large operators insource even painting: UDR built regional in-house painting teams; Petrucci Residential does most painting in-house to cut contractor cost and schedule faster. — [MHN: 6 ways to lower turnover costs](https://www.multihousingnews.com/6-ways-to-lower-resident-turnover-time-costs/); [Multifamily Executive: keeping turnover costs low](https://www.multifamilyexecutive.com/property-management/apartment-trends/keeping-turnover-costs-low_o) (MED)
- Outsourced trades: routine work in-house; HVAC, elevator and fire-system work "typically goes to licensed vendors"; HVAC is "one of the most expensive major mechanical portions of the apartment home." — [Buildium multifamily maintenance guide](https://www.buildium.com/blog/multifamily-maintenance-guide/); [Motili: in-house vs outsourced HVAC](https://www.motili.com/?p=13478) (MED)
- Labor shortage: NAA research (via Lessen, Sep 2025) — 74% of apartment operators cite maintenance staffing shortages as a top operational challenge; onsite maintenance technician turnover 39.2% in 2021 vs 32.7% industry average (NAA); Bisnow survey: staff shortages affecting 24% of multifamily owners/managers; Parks Associates/GE Q4 2022: nearly half find hiring maintenance staff difficult or very difficult. — [Lessen: scaling maintenance support](https://www.lessen.com/resources/scaling-maintenance-support-for-overworked-on-site-teams); [Multifamily Dive: retention](https://multifamilydive.com/news/maintenance-worker-employee-retention/694130); [Bisnow: staff shortages 24%](https://www.bisnow.com/news/national/property-management/maintenance-labor-shortage-pervasive-multifamily-industry-survey-118672) (MED)
- 2026: NAA Apartment Market Pulse shows staffing pressure easing but maintenance hiring still hard; Greystar HR lead: "a shortage of skilled professionals entering the trades." — [Multifamily Dive, 2026](https://www.multifamilydive.com/news/maintenance-staffing-multifamily-labor-challenge/824117/) (MED)
- Centralization: 20for20 report (via AppFolio) — 60% of multifamily operators planning or implementing centralized maintenance; 40% consolidating technology in 2025. — [AppFolio: Lula vendor network](https://www.appfolio.com/articles/lula-vendor-network) (MED)
- Overflow model: Instaservice markets a "Preferred Service Partner" arrangement plus an overflow model for when in-house teams are at capacity; Lessen partnered with Property Meld to supply vendor networks to residential PMs. — [Instaservice partnerships](https://www.instaservice.com/business-solutions/partner-with-us); [Lessen x Property Meld](https://www.lessen.com/resources/lessen-launches-partnership-with-property-meld-to-revolutionize-vendor-management-for-residential-property-managers) (LOW-MED; vendor)
- Utah multifamily scale: RHA Utah (formerly Utah Apartment Association) represents ~3,500 rental operators and 160,000+ units (2026 profile; earlier 132,000); Salt Lake County rental inventory 166,594 units in 2023 (Gardner via UAA deck); Salt Lake City ~64,500 rental units, 6.9% vacancy (2025 RHA report); Utah apartment permits 9,683 units in 2025, more than double 2024. — [UAA Sept 2025 economics deck (PDF)](https://assets.noviams.com/novi-file-uploads/uaa/rha-events/2024_Econ_Data/Woods_Utah_Apartment_Association_Sept_25.pdf); [Utah Business: SLC 7,300 units by 2028](https://www.utahbusiness.com/press-releases/2025/12/22/salt-lake-city-add-7300-rental-units-2028-short-projected-need); [Utah Business: permits 2025](https://www.utahbusiness.com/press-releases/2026/05/13/strong-housing-demand-drives-utah-apartment-permits-third-highest-year-record/) (MED)

### Inferences
- A 200+ unit community already has 2-3 on-site techs; a handyman coordinator is redundant for routine work there. The opening is surge turnover (summer move-out season on the Wasatch Front), vacation/vacancy coverage for the tech role, and sub-licensed small trades the on-site team lacks (drywall, tile, doors).
- The buyer at this tier is a regional maintenance director or community manager with a vendor-management system (Yardi VendorCafe, RealPage, Lessen), not a leasing office — onboarding friction (COI, W-9, portal compliance) is high and pricing is competitive.

### Gaps
- No primary NAA 2024-2026 survey giving techs-per-100-units or outsourcing share was retrievable; 1-per-100 is a 2020 rule of thumb.
- No data on what share of Utah apartment communities are 200+ units or under institutional management.

## Q5. Vendor pain points and practitioner voice (rental PM vs HOA)

### Takeaway
Rental-PM pain centers on speed, repeat visits, communication lag, trip charges, and owner suspicion of markups; HOA pain centers on compliance (insurance/W-9/portal), invoice coding per association, and slow board-driven approvals and payments. Reddit could not be crawled; evidence is from BiggerPockets, Buildium/Property Meld/Latchel/Lula content and HOA management-company blogs.

### Cited Findings
- PM view of vendor failure modes: "slow repairs, repeat visits, unnecessary upgrades, and communication delays usually point to workflow breakdown more than isolated vendor problems." — [BiggerPockets: Is it me or my property manager](https://www.biggerpockets.com/forums/52/topics/691117-is-it-me-or-my-property-manager?page=1) (MED; anecdotal)
- Owner suspicion of PM vendor pricing: "repair quotes by property manager seem extremely high"; PM "doesn't seem to really look into how their vendors are charging." — [BP thread 612814](https://www.biggerpockets.com/forums/600/topics/612814-repair-quotes-by-property-manager-seem-extremely-high?page=1) (MED; anecdotal)

- Property Meld: preferred vendor lists only work when "alive," refreshed weekly on speed-to-accept, chat response time, and time-to-schedule; relying on the same three vendors for a major category correlates with slower (8.2-day) repairs. — [Property Meld: vendor management 2025](https://propertymeld.com/blog/what-we-learned-in-vendor-management-in-2025/) (MED)
- Buildium: PMs source contractors via referrals from people in the business and REALTORS; Landlord Gurus: take referrals "with a grain of salt"; TurboTenant/Azibo: verify insurance and licensing, get multiple quotes. — [Buildium: Making contractors work for you](https://www.buildium.com/blog/making-contractors-work-for-you); [Landlord Gurus: finding handymen](https://landlordgurus.com/how-do-you-find-reliable-handymen-and-contractors-video-extra/); [TurboTenant: find a reliable handyman](https://www.turbotenant.com/property-management/maintenance-management-how-to-find-a-reliable-handyman-in-my-area/); [Azibo: finding a handyman](https://www.azibo.com/blog/finding-a-handyman) (MED)
- Vendor-cost pressure is the #1 rising expense for PM companies (70%). — [Buildium 2026 report blog](https://www.buildium.com/blog/how-property-managers-can-turn-maintenance-into-a-competitive-advantage/) (MED)
- HOA-side vendor pitch (Mr. Handyman to HOA managers): managers want someone who can "work through a queue," carries GL and workers' comp, handles "the documentation your portal requires," and signs vendor agreements. — [Mr. Handyman: HOA property managers](https://www.mrhandyman.com/vero-beach-jupiter/commercial-services/hoa-property-managers/) (LOW; marketing)
- HOA invoices stall when missing approver, operating-vs-reserve coding, or the right association name; management firms require per-community billing profiles. — [InvoiceDataExtraction: HOA AP](https://invoicedataextraction.com/blog/hoa-accounts-payable) (MED)
- HOA approval delay quotes: "a simple bill approval can take several weeks"; board-signature checks delayed when a member travels. — [CSM: bill payments](https://clarksimsonmiller.com/bill-and-invoice-payments/); [PYMNTS: AvidXchange HOA](https://www.pymnts.com/?p=505633) (MED)

### Inferences
- The rental-PM buyer's pain is operational (speed, reliability, communication) and is measured daily in software KPIs — a coordinator can prove value with time-to-schedule and repair-speed numbers.
- The HOA buyer's pain is administrative/fiduciary (compliance, documentation, board optics) — a coordinator proves value by reducing bid-gathering and paperwork for the manager, but cannot shorten the board cycle.

## Q6. Where is the "one vendor who can find a tradesperson for anything" / maintenance-coordination value proposition stronger?

### Takeaway
Evidence of paying for maintenance coordination as a discrete service exists almost entirely on the rental-PM side (Latchel $15-$25/unit/month; Lula flat-rate job catalog integrated into AppFolio; Lessen/Property Meld vendor networks; 60% of multifamily centralizing maintenance). On the HOA side, coordination is treated as part of the management company's fee, and third-party "handyman program" offerings are vendor marketing (Mr. Handyman, Instaservice) rather than a purchased coordination layer.

### Cited Findings
- Latchel sells 24/7 maintenance coordination to PMs at $15-$25/unit/month, month-to-month. — [RentRedi/Latchel](https://rentredi.com/?p=5279) (MED)
- Lula: no monthly fee, flat-rate catalog across plumbing, electrical, HVAC, handyman, seasonal; earns on completed work; AppFolio promotes the Lula vendor network for operators "handling more work orders in-house, renegotiating vendor relationships, and adopting technology." — [Lula Core](https://lula.life/core); [AppFolio: Lula vendor network](https://www.appfolio.com/articles/lula-vendor-network) (MED)
- Lessen partnered with Property Meld to provide vendor networks to residential PMs; Lessen pitches "scaling maintenance support for overworked on-site teams" via outsourcing and vendor networks. — [Lessen x Property Meld](https://www.lessen.com/resources/lessen-launches-partnership-with-property-meld-to-revolutionize-vendor-management-for-residential-property-managers); [Lessen: scaling support](https://www.lessen.com/resources/scaling-maintenance-support-for-overworked-on-site-teams) (MED)
- 60% of multifamily operators planning/implementing centralized maintenance (20for20). — [AppFolio](https://www.appfolio.com/articles/lula-vendor-network) (MED)
- Maintenance is the area renters are most open to automation; 84% expect a response within 24 hours. — [Buildium 2026 Renters Report](https://www.buildium.com/resource/2026-renters-report/) (MED)
- HOA side: the management company itself "can hire and supervise landscapers, janitorial staff, and repair technicians and manage vendor negotiations"; Cedar Management Group lists vendor sourcing with competitive bidding, scheduling and QC inspections as part of its service. — [FirstService MN](https://www.fsresidential.com/minnesota/news-events/articles/demystifying-maintenance-and-repair-roles-for-resi/); [Cedar Management Group](https://www.cedarmanagementgroup.com/?p=626) (MED)
- HOA "handyman program" evidence is limited to franchise/vendor marketing (Mr. Handyman HOA pages; Instaservice preferred-partner/overflow model; MCS "HOA services you should be outsourcing" checklist). — [Mr. Handyman HOA](https://mrhandyman.com/mooresville-lake-norman-concord/commercial-services/hoa-property-maintenance); [Instaservice](https://www.instaservice.com/business-solutions/partner-with-us); [MCS checklist (PDF)](https://commercial.mcs360.com/hubfs/CHECKLIST-THE-HOA-SERVICES-YOU-SHOULD-BE-OUTSOURCING.pdf) (LOW)
- 30-40% of US associations are self-managed (no manager) — these boards have no in-house coordinator at all. — [CAI 2025 Statistical Review](https://foundation.caionline.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/2025StatisticalReviewFoundation.pdf) (HIGH)

### Inferences
- Rental PMs already buy coordination (per-unit subscriptions, flat-rate networks), so the category is validated and the sales conversation is "why you vs Latchel/Lula" — a crowded but proven market.
- HOAs do not buy coordination separately because the management company is paid to do it; the un-served HOA segment is the 30-40% self-managed boards, where a coordinator substitutes for the absent manager — but those boards are small, slow, and payment-process-heavy.
- Tier ranking for a third-party handyman coordinator (inference): (1) small-to-mid single-family/small-multifamily PM firms (~50-1,000 doors) with no in-house techs — steady volume, one decision-maker, software-measured KPIs, tenant clocks; (2) professionally managed HOAs via the management company's vendor list — compliance-heavy, slower pay, lumpier work, but multi-association reach per relationship; (3) self-managed HOAs — unserved but tiny and slow; (4) 200+ unit institutional communities — in-house teams, overflow/turnover only, high onboarding friction.

### Gaps
- No survey quantifying the share of rental PMs that outsource maintenance coordination to a third party, nor any Utah adoption data.
- No evidence found of HOAs or CAM firms purchasing third-party "maintenance coordination" as a line item.

## Q7. Utah specifics: licensing, NARPM Utah, PM company count, tenure split, condo/townhome rental share

### Takeaway
Utah is ~70% owner-occupied / ~30% renter (ACS 2020-2024), with ~326,000-333,000 renter households; about 24% of renters are in detached single-family homes and ~46% in 5+ unit buildings; a 2024 Gardner count puts ~174,551 Utah rental units in SF/townhome/condo/duplex-fourplex stock. Rental PM requires a real-estate or (from 2027) property-manager license; HOA managers are exempt. NARPM Utah sits inside the Rental Housing Association of Utah. No authoritative count of Wasatch Front PM companies exists; directories list 43+ in Salt Lake City alone.

### Cited Findings
- Tenure: Census QuickFacts (ACS 2020-2024 5-yr) — Utah owner-occupied rate 70.2%, 1,124,822 households (implies ~29.8% renter — my arithmetic). - Small-rental stock: Gardner 2024 analysis counts ~174,551 Utah rental units that are single-family, townhome, condo, or duplex/fourplex; 5,722 (3.3%) held by large investors — 2.0% in Salt Lake County, 3.8% in Utah County, 32.3% in Summit County. — [Utah Legislature interim 2025 Gardner presentation (PDF)](https://le.utah.gov/interim/2025/pdf/00002885.pdf) (HIGH)

### Inferences
- Roughly a quarter of Utah renters live in detached single-family homes and ~175,000 units sit in SF/townhome/condo/small-plex stock that is overwhelmingly mom-and-pop owned (97% non-institutional) — exactly the stock that third-party single-family PMs and self-managing landlords control, and that lacks on-site maintenance. - The 2013-2022 shift to 55% attached permits means a growing share of Utah rentals are inside HOAs (townhome/condo), so a coordinator will increasingly touch both buyer types on the same property: the HOA for exterior/common elements, the PM/owner for the interior.
- The Jan 2027 license rollout will likely shake out unlicensed small operators and consolidate doors into licensed PM firms — enlarging the addressable PM-firm segment.
