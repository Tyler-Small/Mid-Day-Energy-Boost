# Handyman Liaison / Maintenance-Coordination Intermediary: Business-Model Benchmarks (US, as of Oct 2026)

Research-method note for the report writer: ~40 web searches were completed; every attempt to fetch full pages (Latchel, Lula, Lessen, le.utah.gov, SEC EDGAR, Franzy, Hinge PDF, FCAR PDF, Atradius, BusinessWire, Startland) failed with DNS errors through the sandbox proxy, and the shared search budget was then exhausted. Findings below therefore rest on search-result excerpts of the cited pages, not on full-page reads. Each key figure carries a confidence label (High = primary source or company filing, Medium = company press/marketing or reputable trade press, Low = third-party aggregator or single secondary source). Anything I could not source is in Gaps, not in findings.

## KQ1. Profiles of comparable models (segment, pricing, take rate, outcomes)

### Takeaway
No comparable company publicly discloses a per-job coordination take rate. The field has split into (a) software-only per-unit/month tools (Property Meld $1.60-$2.00/unit/mo), (b) service-layer coordinators sold to rental property managers on per-unit/month plans (Latchel; multiple conflicting price listings, most credible recent listing $15-$25/unit/mo), (c) flat-rate-catalog coordination with an embedded, undisclosed margin (Lula, $31M raised, 9,000+ pros, 350k+ rental properties), (d) enterprise facilities "we manage the subcontractors" brokers (Lessen/SMS Assist, $950M acquisition; City Wide franchises averaging ~$9.8M revenue/unit), and (e) consumer-facing models that either shrank sharply (Angi Services/Handy: net revenue $118M 2023 -> $72.4M 2025) or moved to W-2 subscription (Honey Homes $295/mo). Nearly every durable coordinator chose B2B property managers or multi-site commercial clients, not HOAs or homeowners; I found no standalone HOA-focused maintenance coordinator.

## KQ2. Industry take rates: PM maintenance markups and facilities-broker margins (City Wide, Lessen, Vixxo, DMG)

### Takeaway
Multiple property-management fee guides converge on a 10%-20% markup (or $25-$50 flat per work order) that owners already tolerate on top of vendor invoices, so a 20% liaison margin sits at the top of the accepted band and will be judged against what the PM itself already charges. City Wide's franchise model proves a pure "manage the subcontractors" B2B business can reach ~$9.8M average revenue per territory, but no gross-margin figure from its Item 19 surfaced.

### Cited Findings
- "Many companies add 10% to 20% on top of vendor invoices to cover their coordination time" — [Bluefield Group](https://www.bluefieldgroup.com/blog/a-transparent-guide-to-property-management-fees-what-landlords-should-expect/). Confidence: Medium.
- Maintenance markup typically 10%-15%; some charge a flat coordination fee of $25-$50 per work order — [Tellus](https://resources.tellusapp.com/passive-income/property-management/property-management-fees). Confidence: Medium.
- Commercial PM: 10%-20% markup on maintenance and repair costs — [HelloData](https://www.hellodata.ai/help-articles/what-are-typical-commercial-property-management-fees). Confidence: Medium.
- Range cited from 0% to 30%+; $20-$100 per work order "less common" — [Reedy & Company](https://www.reedyandcompany.com/blog/how-much-do-property-management-companies-charge-rates-explained/); [Straw Homes](https://www.strawhomes.com/property-management-cost/). Confidence: Low-Medium.
- Owner-side friction: some owners regard markups as "double-dipping" on the monthly management fee; advice is to request actual vendor invoices — [Bluefield Group](https://www.bluefieldgroup.com/blog/a-transparent-guide-to-property-management-fees-what-landlords-should-expect/). Confidence: Medium.
- City Wide Facility Solutions (2026 FDD via Franzy): average gross revenue ~$9.82M per unit; total investment ~$230K-$411K; $70K franchise fee; 5% royalty; 115 franchise units — [Franzy](https://franzy.com/franchises/city-wide-facility-solutions). Confidence: Medium (aggregator citing FDD).
- City Wide: 1% marketing fee; Item 19 earnings claim present; 94 units (count conflicts with Franzy's 115, likely different dates) — [FranchiseOverview](https://franchiseoverview.com/company/city-wide-company). Confidence: Low-Medium.
- City Wide company-reported: H1 2022 average unit volume $6.7M, top-quartile average $15.1M, franchisee median $5.4M; H1 2023 AUV $8.07M; 2023 median $6.4M; systemwide revenue +34% — [ISSA 2022](https://www.issa.com/industry-news/city-wide-reports-strong-first-half-financial-results/); [ISSA 2023](https://www.issa.com/industry-news/city-wide-boosts-annual-revenue-34/); [Franchise Dictionary](https://franchisedictionarymagazine.com/city-wide-facility-solutions-continues-record-growth-with-strong-first-half-of-the-year/). Confidence: Medium (company PR).
- Vixxo: 200,000 vetted providers, 80,000+ locations, 2.2M assets; $112M raised; sells "Full-Service and Guaranteed Rate" contracts that shift downtime risk to Vixxo, contrasting with cost-plus/hourly FM contracts that "reward slow repairs and large invoices"; acquired Cushman & Wakefield's third-party vendor-managed site maintenance business (Aug 2024) — [Vixxo](https://www.vixxo.com/facilities-management-news/why-vixxo-facility-solutions-stands-apart-in-the-facilities-management-market); [PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/13187-17); [Retail & Restaurant Facility Business](https://retailrestaurantfb.com/vixxo-acquires-third-party-vendor-managed-site-maintenance-business-from-cushman-wakefield/). Confidence: Medium.
- Divisions Maintenance Group: PE-backed, Cincinnati; no financial data found — [PitchBook (Vixxo competitors)](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/13187-17). Confidence: Low.
- Angi's implied ~36% gross take on pre-priced consumer jobs (derived above) — [Angi FY2024 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1705110/000170511025000018/angi-20241231.htm). Confidence: High (derived).

### Inferences
- If the PM already charges owners 10-20% on vendor invoices, a liaison adding 20% on top creates a stacked 30-40% load unless the PM waives its markup or the liaison's price is presented as the all-in vendor price. Positioning the 20% inside a flat-rate catalog (Lula's approach) rather than as a visible surcharge is how comparable firms avoid the "double-dipping" objection.
- City Wide's economics (5% royalty + 1% marketing on ~$9.8M AUV, investment well under $0.5M) only work because revenue is recurring janitorial contracts with sub-contracted labor; a job-by-job handyman liaison has far lower revenue predictability per client.
- Vixxo's "Guaranteed Rate" framing shows enterprise buyers pay a coordinator a premium primarily for risk transfer (uptime, price certainty), not for dispatching per se; a guarantee is the monetizable feature.

### Gaps
- City Wide Item 19 gross margin / subcontractor cost percentage not found (FDD behind FRANdata paywall; Franzy page fetch blocked).
- Lessen, Vixxo, DMG gross margins not public.
- No survey quantifying what share of PMs actually charge a markup vs. none.

## KQ3. B2B vs B2C payment risk for small service businesses

### Takeaway
B2B invoicing carries measurable late-payment and bad-debt exposure (roughly half of US B2B invoices overdue, 5-8% written off per Atradius; 59% of US small businesses have invoices 30+ days overdue per QuickBooks 2026; construction averages ~83 days to payment), whereas homeowner work is predominantly paid at point of service (online payments exceeded 50% of Jobber transactions by Q3 2025). I found no dataset directly comparing HOA/PM payment timeliness with homeowners; HOA-specific evidence is anecdotal and points to board-signature and monthly check-run delays.

### Cited Findings
- Atradius US 2024: half of all B2B invoices currently overdue; bad debts average 8% of all B2B credit sales — [Atradius US 2024](https://atradius.us/reports/payment-practices-barometer-b2b-payment-practices-trends-united-states-2024.html). Confidence: High.
- Atradius US 2025 (secondary summary): 52% paid on time, 43% overdue, 5% written off; top cause "customer's own liquidity issues" 45%; survey of 240 US companies — [Lonely Entrepreneur citing Atradius](https://lonelyentrepreneur.com/founder-days-to-get-paid/). Confidence: Low-Medium (not verified on Atradius page).
- Atradius North America Sept 2026: late payment affects seven in ten companies; overdue invoices average 23% of B2B receivables — [Kyodo PR Wire release](https://kyodonewsprwire.jp/release/202609166076). Confidence: Medium.
- Intuit QuickBooks 2026 US Late Payments Report: 59% of small businesses have invoices overdue 30+ days (up from 47% prior year); businesses with unpaid invoices are owed $17.7K on average — [QuickBooks 2026 report](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/r/small-business-data/small-business-late-payments-report-2026/). Confidence: High.
- 2025 US: 56% had unpaid invoices, average $17,500 owed; survey of ~2,500 US small businesses — [Agiled summary](https://agiled.app/statistics/late-payment-statistics). Confidence: Low-Medium (secondary).
- Rabbet 2024 Construction Payments Report: slow payments cost the industry $280B and raised construction costs 14%; 82% of contractors wait >30 days (vs 49% two years earlier); 98% of GCs incur personal financial penalties to float payments — [Rabbet 2024](https://rabbet.com/?p=17167); 2023: $273B — [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20231010985830/en/Delayed-Payments-Added-273-Billion-to-Construction-Bids-in-2023-A-Wake-Up-Call-for-Real-Estate-Developers-and-Construction-Lenders). Confidence: Medium (vendor survey).
- Levelset: construction payment averages 83 days, longest of any industry; 2021 report: subcontractors four times less likely than GCs to be paid within 30 days; 56% of subs waited >60 days for retainage vs 16% of GCs — [JLC Online](https://www.jlconline.com/business/money/levelsets-contractor-profiles-aim-to-identify-slow-paying-contractors_o/); [BD+C](https://bdcnetwork.com/covid-19-made-payment-delays-contractors-much-worse?page=236). Confidence: Medium; dated (2019-2021).
- Jobber Q3 2025 Home Service Economic Report: online payments surpassed 50% of all Jobber transactions — [Landscape Management](https://www.landscapemanagement.net/jobber-releases-its-latest-home-service-economic-report-for-q3/). Confidence: Medium.
- HOA AP: >70% of property vendor payments still by paper check (2018); board-signature requirements cause "significant delays" — [PYMNTS/AvidXchange, 2018](https://www.pymnts.com/startup-check-in/2018/avidxchange-hoa-vertical-ap-automation-payment-methods/). Confidence: Medium, dated.
- Manual HOA approvals cause delays that strain vendor relationships and forfeit early-pay discounts — [Enumerate](https://goenumerate.com/blog/breaking-through-the-ap-bottleneck). Confidence: Low (vendor blog).
- Anecdote: Buda, TX HOA vendors "going 4-6 weeks at best before receiving payment" — [Hays Free Press](https://www.haysfreepress.com/article/26694,buda-hoas-claim-mismanagement-led-to-financial-burden). Confidence: Low (allegation).
- Contractor forum: HOAs and management companies run payments on fixed monthly cycles so net-30 often lands later — [Pressure Washing Resource forum](https://pressurewashingresource.com/community/t/trying-to-learn-how-to-write-a-net-30/16156). Confidence: Low.

### Inferences
- A liaison that pays its 1099 handymen promptly (Lula's "Fast Pay") while billing HOA/PM clients on net-30-plus terms must finance 30-60 days of working capital on 100% of job value while earning only the ~20% margin; at 480 associations this float, not the margin, is the binding constraint. Lula's Fast Pay and Jobox's embedded credit line are both responses to exactly this gap.
- Homeowner (B2C) work has lower credit risk but higher acquisition cost and dispute/chargeback exposure; B2B has lower CAC per dollar but concentrated receivable risk (one PM client = many associations).

### Gaps
- No study comparing HOA/PM vs homeowner days-to-pay or bad-debt rates; CAI/NARPM have not published this as far as search results show.
- Jobber's HSER does not break out late payments by residential vs commercial.
- Could not verify the Atradius 2025 US figures on Atradius's own site (fetch blocked).

## KQ4. Niche vs generalist: evidence on specialization in B2B services

### Takeaway
The best available evidence (Hinge's longitudinal High Growth Study and BCG's 2023 verticalization survey) consistently associates specialization with faster growth, higher profitability and buyer preference, but it is survey-based and self-reported; I found no peer-reviewed or measured win-rate evidence specific to trades or property-services firms.

### Cited Findings
- Hinge 2016: high-growth firms (20%+ annual growth) are 75% more likely to have a highly specialized practice — [Hinge](https://hingemarketing.com/?p=19114). Confidence: Medium (vendor research).
- Hinge 2017: high-growth firms "much more likely to target a highly specialized niche" and are 50% more profitable than average-growth peers — [Hinge 2017 study](https://hingemarketing.com/blog/story/2017-high-growth-research-report-released); [2017 PDF](https://www.hingemarketing.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/2017-High-Growth-Study.pdf). Confidence: Medium.
- Hinge 2018: high-growth firms more specialized in four areas including specialized services and serving a specific role; low-growth firms leaned toward industry-only specialization — [Hinge](https://hingemarketing.com/?p=24417). Confidence: Medium.
- Secondary summary: high-growth firms focusing on specialization, technology and marketing achieve median annual growth >34% vs no-growth firms — [Hinge/National Law Review](https://hingemarketing.com/about-hinge/news-events/news/article/the-national-law-review-highlights-hinges-high-growth-study). Confidence: Low (secondary).
- Hinge 2025: professional-services growth declined for the first time in four years, back to pre-pandemic levels — [Advisorpedia](https://www.advisorpedia.com/research/2025-high-growth-study-key-insights-from-top-performing-firms). Confidence: Medium.
- BCG 2023 (105 software sellers, 125 buyers, large North American firms): up to 80% of executive-level buyers say verticalized solutions have moderate to very high impact on purchasing decisions — [BCG](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2023/how-verticalization-is-accelerating-and-what-that-means-for-software-companies). Confidence: Medium (software, not services).
- Agency anecdote: one mid-sized agency saw a 38% win-rate lift in focus verticals within 12 months of verticalizing — [Pedowitz Group](https://www.pedowitzgroup.com/agencies-segment-clients-by-industry-vertical?hsLang=en). Confidence: Low (single case, vendor).
- Unverified secondary claims: 35% pricing premium for vertical expertise, 67% higher win rate for sub-specialists — [Starr Conspiracy](https://www.thestarrconspiracy.com/insights/benchmarks/b2b-fintech-marketing-agency-benchmark). Confidence: Low.

### Inferences
- Hinge's 2018 finding that low-growth firms skewed to industry-only specialization while high-growth firms combined service + role specialization suggests "HOA managers" (industry) alone is weaker positioning than "maintenance coordination for HOA community managers" (industry + role + service).

### Gaps

## KQ6. Scalability constraints: handyman supply, Utah licensing, 1099 rules, liability, quality control

### Takeaway
Utah's handyman exemption appears to have moved from $3,000 to $7,000 per project under HB 483 (2024), with a DOPL "affirmation of exemption" filing for the $3,000-$7,000 tier and electrical/plumbing/HVAC always excluded; Utah also has a 2018 Service Marketplace Platforms Act that presumes building-service contractors on a platform are independent contractors for jobs priced at $3,000 or less. Comparable firms solved supply and quality either by W-2 employment (Honey Homes), by tight vetting/insurance requirements plus fast pay (Lula), or by risk-transfer contracts (Vixxo).

### Cited Findings
- HB 483 (2024), "Construction Trade Amendments," amends Utah Code 58-55-305; the substitute bill text strikes $3,000 and inserts $7,000 in subsection (1)(h)(i) (intermediate drafts showed $35,000 and $9,000) — [HB 483 substitute text](https://le.utah.gov/~2024/bills/hbillamd/HB0483S01.htm); [enrolled PDF](https://le.utah.gov/~2024/bills/hbillenr/HB0483.pdf); [current 58-55-305 (eff. 5/7/2025)](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title58/Chapter55/C58-55-S305_2025050720250507.html). Confidence: Medium-High (statutory text seen only in search excerpt; full fetch blocked). The user's premise of a $3,000 cap appears to be pre-2024 law.
- Secondary 2026 guide: no state license for handyman work under $7,000 (labor and materials); work $3,000-$7,000 requires filing a DOPL affirmation of exemption; $7,000+ requires a licensed contractor; electrical, plumbing and HVAC always require their own trade license — [ContractorLicenseRequirements.com](https://contractorlicenserequirements.com/utah/handyman-license-requirements/). Confidence: Medium.
- Older sources still describe a $3,000 cap (labor + materials), with DOPL affirmation-of-exemption registration ($35 fee) above $1,000; no building permit or licensed trades allowed under the exemption — [Next Insurance](https://www.nextinsurance.com/blog/utah-handyman-license-and-insurance-requirements/); [Handyman Startup](https://www.handymanstartup.com/handyman-license-utah/); city handouts from Ogden and Kaysville show the older tiers — [Kaysville PDF](https://www.kaysville.gov/DocumentCenter/View/195/Handyman-Information-PDF). Confidence: Medium, dated.
- Springville, UT city page lists the $7,000 cap — [Springville](https://www.springvilleutah.gov/community-development/?p=10088). Confidence: Medium.
- Utah Service Marketplace Platforms Act (Utah Code Title 34, Ch. 53; HB 364, 2018): covers "building services" priced at $3,000 or less (home repair, cleaning, moving, landscaping); a building service contractor affiliated with a platform is presumed an independent contractor unless clear and convincing evidence shows the parties intended employment; contractor may affiliate as IC or employee — [HB 364 enrolled](https://le.utah.gov/~2018/bills/hbillenr/HB0364.pdf); [Utah Code 34-53-102](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title34/Chapter53/C34-53-S102_2018050820180508.pdf). Confidence: High (statute), though current amendments not re-verified.
- Honey Homes chose W-2 handypeople with benefits and training to control quality in a subscription model — [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/this-startup-wants-you-to-radically-reimagine-the-local-handyman/). Confidence: Medium.
- Lula requires pros to be "licensed, insured and vetted" and offers Fast Pay to attract supply — [lula.life](https://lula.life/core). Confidence: Medium.
- Vixxo's Guaranteed Rate contracts shift downtime risk to the coordinator — [Vixxo](https://www.vixxo.com/facilities-management-news/why-vixxo-facility-solutions-stands-apart-in-the-facilities-management-market). Confidence: Medium.
- Inframark qualifies contractors on credentials, insurance and past performance and tracks work orders/invoices as part of HOA management — [Inframark](https://www.inframark.com/solutions/community-management/arizona/association-management/vendor-contract-oversight/). Confidence: Medium.

### Inferences
- The Utah platform-IC presumption ($3,000 cap per job) and the handyman licensing exemption ($7,000 cap, with affirmation above $3,000) together define a legally cleaner operating envelope of jobs under $3,000 for a 1099 dispatch model; jobs between $3,000 and $7,000 need DOPL affirmation and fall outside the platform safe harbor; anything involving electrical/plumbing/HVAC needs a licensed trade sub regardless.
- The federal DOL independent-contractor test status for 2025-26 was not researched (budget); treat federal classification as an open risk item.

### Gaps
- Could not read the enrolled HB 483 or current 58-55-305 text to confirm the final $7,000 figure and whether the "affirmation" tier is $3,000-$7,000 (two secondary sources agree).
- Federal DOL 2024 IC rule status and any 2025-26 rescission; vicarious/negligent-hiring liability case law for coordinators whose vendor damages property; cost of quality-control (re-work rates) at comparable firms — none retrieved.

## KQ7. Unit-economics templates: spend per manager relationship, per HOA, per door

### Takeaway
FCAR data imply the average US association has ~78 units and collects ~$4,300 per unit per year in assessments (2024-25), of which roughly 60-70% is operating budget; SFR benchmarks put repair & maintenance at 5-15% of rent with median work orders of $327-$707. For 20 management clients x 24 associations (480 associations), total assessments under management are on the order of $160M, but the handyman-addressable slice is a small fraction and no published figure isolates it.

### Cited Findings
- FCAR 2025 Statistical Review: 2024 = 369,000 associations, 28.8M housing units, 77.1M residents; $124.2B assessments reported in the 2025 review; FCAR estimates 374,000-377,000 associations in 2026 — [FCAR 2025 Statistical Review PDF](https://foundation.caionline.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/2025StatisticalReviewFoundation.pdf); [FCAR statistical review page](https://foundation.caionline.org/publications/factbook/statistical-review/). Confidence: High (association data), Medium on which year the $124.2B applies to (excerpt ambiguous).
- 2023 assessments $108.8B — [FCAR 2023 review](https://foundation.caionline.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/2023StatsReviewDigital-002.pdf); 2024 estimate $120.9B (secondary) — [HOPB](https://www.hopb.co/hoa-statistics). Confidence: High / Low respectively.
- Mix: HOAs ~58-63%, condominiums ~35-40%, cooperatives 2-4% of associations — [PNW Residences summary](https://www.pnwresidences.com/blog/hoa-statistics-2026/). Confidence: Low-Medium.
- Typical HOA budget split: operating expenses 60-70% (landscaping, utilities, insurance, staffing, trash), reserve contributions 30-40% — [Gerald citing NerdWallet](https://joingerald.com/learn/money-basics/hoa-money). Confidence: Medium.
- SFR R&M = 5%-15% of rental income; median work-order costs $327-$707 (general maintenance, HVAC, plumbing), 2024 data — [Lessen benchmark release](https://lessen.com/resources/lessen-releases-first-ever-repair-maintenance-benchmark-report-setting-a-new-standard-for-the-single-family-rental-industry). Confidence: Medium.
- Practitioner rule of thumb: ~7% of income for SFR maintenance; $400-$500 per unit per year for multifamily — [BiggerPockets user post](https://biggerpockets.com/posts/user/snb778). Confidence: Low.
- Homeowner (owner-occupied) benchmark: Thumbtack estimated $11,196/yr to maintain a single-family home with hired pros (Q2 2026) — cited via [Baselane](https://www.baselane.com/resources/rental-property-maintenance-cost-statistics). Confidence: Low-Medium (secondary).
- Lula: 350,000+ properties and 9,000+ pros, i.e., roughly 39 properties per pro — [lula.life](https://lula.life/core). Confidence: Medium (derived).

### Inferences (explicitly assumption-driven; the report writer should present as a scenario, not a finding)
- Average association ≈ 28.8M / 369,000 ≈ 78 units; assessments ≈ $124.2B / ~28.8-29M units ≈ $4,300 per unit per year; ≈ $335K per average association per year.
- 480 associations x $335K ≈ $161M total assessments; at 60-70% operating ≈ $97M-$113M operating spend across the portfolio.
- Handyman-addressable common-area repair work (excluding landscaping/pool/insurance/utilities/management contracts) is unquantified in any source found; if it were 5-10% of operating spend (assumption, no citation), the addressable pool would be ~$5M-$11M/yr; capturing 10-20% of that gives ~$0.5M-$2.3M of coordinated spend, i.e., ~$100K-$450K gross margin at 20%. Using Lessen's $327-$707 median work order, that volume equals roughly 1,000-5,000 work orders per year across 480 associations (about 2-10 per association per year).
- Per management-client relationship (24 associations): ~$8M total assessments, ~$5M operating; coordinated spend plausibly $25K-$115K/yr under the same assumptions, i.e., $5K-$23K gross margin per client per year at 20%.

### Gaps
- No source gives repair-and-maintenance (vs landscaping, insurance, utilities) as a share of HOA operating budgets; CAI/FCAR budget-composition data were not retrievable (PDF fetch blocked).
- No published "maintenance spend per association" or "per door" for HOAs; SFR per-door annual figures were not in the Lessen excerpt.
